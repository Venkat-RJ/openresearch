# Problem Description

A delivery company operates a fleet of homogeneous vehicles stationed at a central depot to serve a set of geographically dispersed customers. Each customer has a known demand, a service time window specifying the earliest and latest allowable times at which service may begin, and a preferred service time equal to the midpoint of its time window. Each customer also has a coordinate location in the plane. The depot has its own time window representing the planning horizon during which all vehicles must depart and return. The number of available vehicles and the capacity of each vehicle are given.

The input data includes, for every ordered pair of locations (including the depot as both origin and destination), a travel time and a routing cost, both derived from Euclidean distances between coordinate positions and rounded to one decimal place. It also gives a nonnegative service duration for every customer; a vehicle may depart a customer only after that service duration has elapsed. Each customer's inconvenience cost is a quadratic function of the service start time at that customer: it equals an inconvenience cost factor multiplied by the square of the difference between the actual service start time and the customer's preferred time. The inconvenience cost is therefore zero when service begins exactly at the preferred time and increases as the service start time deviates in either direction. The depot vertices carry no inconvenience cost.

The company must select a set of vehicle routes, where each route starts at the depot, visits a sequence of customers, and returns to the depot. Along each route, the company must also determine the service start time at every visited customer. Every customer must appear on exactly one route. The total demand of customers on any single route must not exceed the vehicle capacity. The number of routes used must not exceed the number of available vehicles. Along each route, the service start time at each stop must fall within that stop's time window, and the service start time at each successive stop must be no earlier than the previous service start plus the previous customer's service duration and the travel time between the two stops.

The goal is to minimize the total cost, which is the sum of two components: the total routing cost, computed as the sum of arc routing costs over all arcs traversed by all routes, and the total inconvenience cost, computed as the sum over all customers of the quadratic inconvenience cost evaluated at each customer's scheduled service start time.
