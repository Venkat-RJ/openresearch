# Problem Description

A region contains a set of customers, a set of lower-level facilities (level I), and a set of upper-level facilities (level II), all positioned at known coordinates in a two-dimensional plane. The system is a capacitated nested hierarchical service network: level II facilities can provide both type I and type II services, while level I facilities can provide only type I service. Each customer has a known demand, a fixed fraction theta of which requires type I service and the remaining fraction (one minus theta) requires type II service. Additionally, of the type I demand that any facility serves, a fixed fraction sigma generates a subsequent need for referential type II service, which must be forwarded to a level II facility or outsourced.

An interdictor (attacker) and a defender operate in sequence. The interdictor acts first and selects an interdiction intensity level for every facility. The set of possible intensity levels ranges from zero (no attack) through a maximum level; exactly one intensity level must be assigned to each facility. Each intensity level at each facility tier has an associated attack cost, and the total attack expenditure across all facilities must not exceed a given interdiction budget. When a level I facility is interdicted at a given intensity, its type I service capacity is reduced by the corresponding level I reduction ratio applied to its original type I capacity. When a level II facility is interdicted at a given intensity, its type I capacity is reduced by the level II reduction ratio, and its type II capacity is similarly reduced by the same level II reduction ratio. At intensity zero the reduction ratios are zero, so no capacity is lost.

After the interdictor commits to an interdiction pattern, the defender allocates customer demand to the surviving capacity of the facilities and may also outsource unmet demand. The defender determines, for each customer, how much of that customer's type I demand is sent to each level I or level II facility and how much is outsourced, and similarly how much of that customer's type II demand is sent to each level II facility and how much is outsourced. For referential type II demand, the defender determines how much of each facility's generated referral demand is forwarded to each level II facility and how much is outsourced. A customer's type I demand fraction must be fully covered by the sum of allocations to facilities (level I or level II) and the outsourced amount. Likewise, a customer's type II demand fraction must be fully covered by allocations to level II facilities plus outsourcing. For every facility, the referential type II demand it generates (sigma times the total type I demand it serves) must equal the sum of its referral flows to level II facilities plus the outsourced referral amount for that facility. Furthermore, any type I demand that a customer outsources also generates referential type II demand at the rate sigma, and this referral demand is automatically outsourced as well.

The total type I demand served by a level I facility must not exceed that facility's original type I capacity minus the capacity lost to interdiction. The total type I demand served by a level II facility must not exceed that facility's original type I capacity minus the capacity lost to interdiction (using the level II reduction ratio). The combined type II demand and incoming referral flows served by a level II facility must not exceed that facility's original type II capacity minus the capacity lost to interdiction.

The cost the defender incurs has several components. Allocating type I demand from a customer to a level I facility costs alpha per unit of demand per unit of distance between them. Allocating type I demand from a customer to a level II facility costs beta per unit of demand per unit of distance. Allocating type II demand from a customer to a level II facility also costs beta per unit of demand per unit of distance. Forwarding referral demand from any facility to a level II facility costs gamma per unit of demand per unit of distance between those two facilities. Outsourcing type I demand costs alpha-prime per unit. Outsourcing type II demand costs beta-prime per unit. Outsourcing referral demand at a facility costs gamma-prime per unit. Outsourced type I demand also incurs an additional referral outsourcing charge of sigma times gamma-prime per unit of outsourced type I demand.

The interdictor's goal is to choose the interdiction pattern that maximizes the defender's minimum total cost. The defender's goal is to choose the demand allocation and outsourcing plan that minimizes the total cost, given the interdiction pattern selected by the interdictor. The overall problem is therefore to find the interdiction pattern and the corresponding optimal defender response that together yield the maximum possible minimum cost.
