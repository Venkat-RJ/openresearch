# Problem Description

A single vehicle must serve n customer requests, where each request consists of picking up one unit-size item at a location in a pickup region and delivering it to a corresponding location in a separate delivery region. The vehicle must complete all pickups before beginning any deliveries. Specifically, the vehicle starts at a pickup depot, visits each of the n pickup locations exactly once, returns to the pickup depot, then travels to a delivery depot, visits each of the n delivery locations exactly once, and returns to the delivery depot. The travel cost from the pickup depot to the delivery depot is zero.

The pickup region is described by a complete directed graph whose vertices are the pickup depot plus the n pickup locations, and the delivery region is described by a separate complete directed graph whose vertices are the delivery depot plus the n delivery locations. Each arc in each graph has a given routing cost of zero or greater. The input provides the full distance matrix for each region, computed as rounded Euclidean distances from the coordinates of the vertices.

The vehicle has a loading area divided into s stacks, each of which can hold at most l items. Items are placed on top of a stack during pickup and removed from the top of a stack during delivery, following a last-in-first-out policy. This means that if one request is picked up before another and both items are loaded into the same stack, then the later-picked-up item must be delivered before the earlier-picked-up item.

The planner must determine a cyclic route through the pickup region that starts and ends at the pickup depot and visits every pickup location exactly once, a cyclic route through the delivery region that starts and ends at the delivery depot and visits every delivery location exactly once, and an assignment of each item to a stack, such that the loading plan satisfies the stack capacities and the last-in-first-out unloading requirement. Each pickup location and the pickup depot must have exactly one arc entering it and exactly one arc leaving it within the pickup route, and likewise each delivery location and the delivery depot must have exactly one arc entering it and exactly one arc leaving it within the delivery route. Both routes must form single connected cycles, meaning no subset of two or more vertices may form a closed loop disconnected from the rest of the route. Any pair of partial pickup and delivery paths for which there is no way to load the items common to both paths into the s stacks of height l while respecting the last-in-first-out policy is load-incompatible and may not appear simultaneously in the chosen routes; that is, for any such pair of sub-paths, at least one arc from their combined arc set must be absent from the solution. The goal is to minimize the total routing cost, which is the sum of all arc costs traversed in the pickup route plus all arc costs traversed in the delivery route.
