# Problem Description

A delivery company operates an unlimited fleet of identical vehicles, each with the same carrying capacity, from a single depot. The company must deliver goods to a set of customers, where each customer has a known positive demand, a location specified by coordinates in a two-dimensional plane, a time window defined by an earliest and latest allowable start of service, and a service time representing how long the vehicle must spend at that customer once service begins. The depot has zero demand and zero service time, and its time window spans the entire planning horizon from time zero to a given horizon length. Travel costs and travel times between every pair of locations are given; in particular, they equal the Euclidean distance between the two locations.

The company must design a set of vehicle routes, where each route begins at the depot, visits a sequence of one or more customers, and returns to the depot. Every customer must appear on exactly one route. A direct connection from one location to another is only available if the combined demands of the two endpoints do not exceed the vehicle capacity and if a vehicle that begins service at the origin at the earliest allowable time, completes service, and travels directly to the destination can arrive no later than the destination's latest allowable service start time. Each route must satisfy three requirements: the total demand of all customers on the route must not exceed the vehicle capacity; at each stop along the route, service must begin within the customer's time window, where the earliest possible start of service at each successive stop is the later of that customer's earliest time and the previous customer's service start plus that customer's service time plus the travel time between the two customers; and the vehicle must return to the depot within the planning horizon, meaning the departure time from the last customer (its service start plus its service time) plus the travel time back to the depot must not exceed the horizon length. A vehicle may arrive at a customer before the opening of the customer's time window and wait, but it may not begin service after the closing of the time window.

The goal is to select a collection of routes that satisfy these requirements, one for each vehicle used, such that every customer is served by exactly one route and the total cost, defined as the sum of the travel costs along all connections used across all selected routes, is minimized.
