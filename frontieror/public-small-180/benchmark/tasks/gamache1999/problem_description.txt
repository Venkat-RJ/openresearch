# Problem Description

An airline must construct personalized monthly schedules, called rosters, for each of its cabin crew members over a planning horizon of a fixed number of days (typically 30). The airline has a set of crew members and a set of pairings to be covered. A pairing is a sequence of flight segments on consecutive days, worked by a crew leaving and returning to a home base. Each pairing has a known duration in minutes, a start day within the planning horizon, a span in days, a departure hour, a return hour, a total number of flight hours, an aircraft type, a flag indicating whether it is a cross-pairing (departing from one base and arriving at another), and a coverage requirement specifying how many crew members must be assigned to fly that pairing. Each crew member has a set of aircraft-type qualifications and required visas determining which pairings he or she may fly, a list of pre-assigned activities with their start days, end days, durations, and types (such as annual leave, training, medical appointments, reserve blocks, transition activities carrying over from the previous or into the next month, and desiderata), and a record of how many flight hours the crew member accumulated during the last fifteen days of the preceding month.

The airline must choose exactly one roster for each crew member. A roster is a feasible assignment of pairings, rest periods, and pre-assigned activities across the days of the month. Every pre-assigned activity must appear in the crew member's roster on its specified days, and no two activities may overlap in time within any roster. A crew member may only be assigned pairings consistent with his or her aircraft-type qualifications and visa requirements, and a crew member may only fly pairings that do not overlap with that crew member's pre-assigned activities. Each pairing must be covered by exactly the number of crew members specified by its coverage requirement; any shortfall is recorded as the number of copies of that pairing left uncovered.

Rosters must satisfy a collection of work rules derived from the collective agreement. A crew member is entitled to a weekly rest period of at least 48 hours during each week, where a week runs from Monday at 00:00 to Sunday at 24:00; no weekly rest period may begin on Sunday after 06:00 (equivalently, the rest must start no later than Sunday at 06:00 so that it can be completed within the week or overflow by at most 30 hours into the following week). Once per month, an additional 48 hours must be added to one of the weekly rest periods to form a monthly rest period; this additional 48-hour requirement may be reduced by 12 hours for every 7 inactive days among the crew member's pre-assigned activities that are not flight activities. No more than eight 24-hour periods may elapse between two consecutive periodic breaks (weekly or monthly rests) or between a periodic break and an annual leave period or similar qualifying rest; this limit increases to nine 24-hour periods if the interval includes a reserve block. An employee may not fly more than 77 hours in a single month; additionally, the total flight hours accumulated between the 16th of the preceding month and the 15th of the current month must not exceed 85 hours, and the flight hours already flown during the last 15 days of the preceding month (given as input for each crew member) count toward this rolling limit. Departures before 08:00 may not occur on more than two consecutive days. A crew member may be assigned at most one cross-pairing per month. All reserved blocks must be followed by periodic rests or pairings that depart at noon or later.

The goal is to minimize the total duration, measured in minutes, of all uncovered pairings across the entire month, which is equivalent to maximizing the total duration of pairings covered by the regular crew. This total uncovered duration is computed as the sum, over all pairings, of the pairing's duration in minutes multiplied by the number of uncovered copies of that pairing (the difference between the coverage requirement and the number of crew members actually assigned to it). Equivalently, the airline may minimize the total unproductive time across all selected rosters, where unproductive time on a roster is the time during which the crew member is neither flying a pairing nor engaged in a pre-assigned activity; these two objectives differ only by a constant determined by the schedule length, the total pre-assigned activity duration, and the total duration of all pairings weighted by their coverage requirements.
