# Problem Description

A manufacturing facility has a set of jobs that must be processed on a given number of identical parallel machines. Each job has a known integer processing time, a positive integer weight reflecting its importance, and an integer due date by which it should ideally be completed. Each machine can process only one job at a time, and once a job begins processing on a machine it must run to completion without interruption or transfer to another machine. Every job must be processed exactly once on exactly one machine.

Time is divided into unit-length periods from zero up to a planning horizon. The planning horizon is computed by taking the total processing time of all jobs, subtracting the largest individual processing time, dividing the result by the number of machines, taking the floor of that quotient, and then adding back the largest individual processing time. All machines are available starting at time zero. The scheduler must determine which machine each job is assigned to, the order in which jobs are processed on each machine, and the exact integer start time of each job, such that no two jobs overlap on the same machine. A job finishes at its start time plus its processing time. Idle time between consecutive jobs on the same machine is permitted.

The tardiness of a job is defined as the amount by which its completion time exceeds its due date, or zero if the job finishes on or before its due date. The weighted tardiness of a job is the product of its weight and its tardiness. The goal is to find a schedule that minimizes the total weighted tardiness summed over all jobs.
