# Problem Description

A set of ships arrives at an inland waterway dam equipped with a lock and a quay. The lock consists of a single rectangular chamber with a known width and length, and the quay is divided into several berths. Each ship has a known width, length, arrival time at a coordination point, and a transshipment penalty reflecting the extra cost and unwillingness associated with choosing to unload cargo at the quay rather than passing through the lock. For each ship, a set of other ships to which it is allowed to moor inside the lock chamber is given, as well as a set of berths at which it may be served if it chooses the transshipment mode. The handling time of each ship at each eligible berth is given. Each berth has a start and end of availability time. The lock chamber has a start of availability time. A fixed duration is required for each lockage, and a minimum time interval must elapse between the completion of one lockage and the completion of the next. Some ships may be restricted to pass only through the lock, and others may be restricted to use only the transshipment mode. A sufficiently large time horizon is given.

For each ship, the operator must choose one of two modes: lock mode, in which the ship passes through the lock chamber, or transshipment mode, in which the ship's cargo is handled at a quay berth. The number of potential lockages equals the number of ships, serving as an upper bound; each lockage may or may not be used. The number of service order positions at each berth also equals the number of ships. For each lock-mode ship, the operator must assign it to exactly one lockage and determine its position inside the chamber, specified by whole-number x and y coordinates representing the ship's placement within the chamber's width and length. For each transshipment-mode ship, the operator must assign it to exactly one berth and one service order position at that berth. The operator must also determine the completion time of each lockage and the departure time of each ship.

Ships assigned to the same lockage must not overlap when placed inside the chamber. Specifically, for any two ships in the same lockage, at least one of the following must hold: one ship is entirely to the left of the other in the width direction, or one ship is entirely behind the other in the length direction. If one ship is entirely to the left of another, the first ship's x-coordinate plus its width must not exceed the second ship's x-coordinate. If one ship is entirely behind another, the first ship's y-coordinate plus its length must not exceed the second ship's y-coordinate. Every ship placed in the chamber must fit within the chamber dimensions: its x-coordinate plus its width must not exceed the chamber width, and its y-coordinate plus its length must not exceed the chamber length.

Each lock-mode ship must be assigned to exactly one lockage. A lockage can only be marked as used if at least one ship is assigned to it, and a ship can only be assigned to a lockage that is used. Each lock-mode ship must be moored to at least one entity: either to the left side of another allowed ship, to the right side of another allowed ship, to the left wall of the chamber, or to the right wall of the chamber. A ship moored to the left side of another ship must be adjacent to it: the mooring ship must be contained within the length span of the ship it moors to, and their x-coordinates must be such that the mooring ship's right edge touches the other ship's left edge. The same adjacency logic applies symmetrically for mooring to the right side, where the mooring ship must be contained within the length span of the ship it moors to, and the other ship's right edge must touch the mooring ship's left edge. For mooring to the chamber's right wall, the ship's right edge must touch the wall, with analogous conditions for the chamber's left wall. If one ship moors to the left side of another, the second ship cannot simultaneously moor to the right side of the first. Two ships that are not assigned to the same lockage cannot moor to each other. Two ships can only be in the same lockage if both choose the lock mode.

The departure time of a lock-mode ship equals the completion time of the lockage that transfers it. The departure time of a transshipment-mode ship equals its arrival time. The completion time of each lockage minus the lockage duration must be no earlier than the chamber's start of availability time, and also no earlier than the arrival time of every ship assigned to that lockage. The minimum time interval between consecutive lockage completions must be respected. Lockages must be ordered: if a lockage index is used, all lower-indexed lockages must also be used, and completion times must be nondecreasing across lockage indices. A first-come-first-served rule applies to lock-mode ships: among any two ships that both choose the lock mode, the one arriving earlier must depart no later than the one arriving later. Furthermore, for any two lock-mode ships where one arrives earlier, the earlier-arriving ship must be assigned to the same or an earlier lockage than the later-arriving ship.

Each transshipment-mode ship must be assigned to exactly one berth and one service order position at that berth. Each service order position at each berth may serve at most one ship. When a ship is assigned to a particular service order at a berth, idle time may be incurred at the berth before the ship's service begins. Specifically, the cumulative handling times and idle times of all ships served at that berth in earlier service orders, plus the idle time before the current ship, must be at least as large as the difference between the ship's arrival time and the berth's start of availability time. The total handling time and idle time across all service orders at a berth must not exceed the berth's total available time window.

The goal is to minimize the sum of three components: the total delay of lock-mode ships, where each lock-mode ship's delay is its departure time minus its arrival time; the total delay of transshipment-mode ships, where each such ship's delay accounts for the berth's start time, the cumulative handling and idle times of ships served before it at the same berth, its own handling and idle time, minus its arrival time; and the total transshipment penalty summed over all ships that choose the transshipment mode.
