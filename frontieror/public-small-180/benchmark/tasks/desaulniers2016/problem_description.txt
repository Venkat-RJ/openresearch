# Problem Description

A single supplier operates a depot that produces a known quantity of a single commodity in each period of a finite planning horizon. The supplier must distribute this commodity to a set of customers using a homogeneous fleet of vehicles, each with a common capacity. The planning horizon consists of a fixed number of discrete periods, and the set of locations comprises the depot (indexed zero) and all customers. The input data specifies, for every pair of locations, a symmetric travel cost derived from the rounded Euclidean distance between their coordinates. Each customer has a known demand (consumption) in each period, an inventory capacity, an initial inventory level that does not exceed that capacity, and a per-unit holding cost charged on inventory remaining at the end of each period. The depot likewise has an inventory capacity, an initial inventory that does not exceed that capacity, and a per-unit holding cost.

The supplier must decide, for each period, which subset of customers to visit, how much to deliver to each visited customer, and how to combine those visits into vehicle routes that start and end at the depot. Within each period, the supplier first adds its production to depot inventory, then dispatches vehicles to make deliveries, and customers receive their deliveries at the start of the period so that the delivered goods are available to satisfy that period's demand. No customer may experience a stockout in any period, meaning that the inventory at every customer at the end of every period must remain nonnegative. Similarly, the depot inventory at the end of every period must be nonnegative and must not exceed the depot's inventory capacity.

The inventory at each customer at the end of a period equals its inventory at the end of the previous period plus the total quantity delivered to it in the current period minus its demand in the current period. The same balance applies at the depot, where inventory equals the previous period's ending inventory plus the current period's production minus the total quantity shipped out across all vehicles in the current period. The quantity delivered to a customer in any period may be any nonnegative amount, subject to the restriction that the customer's inventory immediately after receiving the delivery (that is, the previous ending inventory plus the delivery quantity) must not exceed the customer's inventory capacity. Each customer may be visited by at most one vehicle in any given period. The total quantity loaded onto any single vehicle in a given period, summed across all customers it visits on its route, must not exceed the vehicle capacity. In each period, the number of routes operated must not exceed the number of available vehicles. Every route must form a simple tour beginning and ending at the depot, visiting each customer on the route exactly once, and no subtours among customers are permitted.

The goal is to minimize the total cost, defined as the sum of two components: the total travel cost, obtained by summing the travel costs of all arcs traversed by all vehicles across all periods, and the total holding cost, obtained by summing the per-unit holding cost times the ending inventory at every location (depot and all customers) in every period.
