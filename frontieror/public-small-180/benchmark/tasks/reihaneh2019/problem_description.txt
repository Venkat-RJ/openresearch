# Problem Description

A distribution company operates a fleet of identical vehicles from a single central depot to deliver goods to geographically dispersed customers. Rather than visiting every customer directly, the company selects a subset of candidate delivery sites (such as parking lots or community centers) where customers travel to pick up their orders. The company must simultaneously decide which delivery sites to activate, which customers to assign to which activated sites, and how to route vehicles through the activated sites, so that total cost is minimized.

The problem involves three types of entities: a central depot (labeled node zero), a set of candidate delivery sites, and a set of customers. Each customer has a known positive integer demand measured in pallets. The input data specifies the Euclidean-distance-based travel cost between every pair of nodes in the routing network (the depot and all candidate delivery sites) and the Euclidean-distance-based assignment cost between every customer and every candidate delivery site. All vehicles share a common capacity, expressed in pallets. The number of available vehicles is set to two more than the ceiling of total customer demand divided by vehicle capacity.

The company must make three interrelated sets of choices. First, it must decide which candidate delivery sites to open. Second, it must assign every customer to exactly one opened delivery site served by exactly one vehicle tour; no customer may be left unserved. Third, it must design vehicle tours that begin at the central depot, visit a sequence of opened delivery sites to drop off goods, and return to the depot.

Every delivery site may be visited by at most one vehicle tour, so no two tours share a delivery site. Each vehicle tour traverses at most one route segment leaving the depot. For every node in the network (depot or delivery site), the number of route segments entering it on a given tour must equal the number of route segments leaving it on that tour, ensuring each tour forms a closed loop through the depot.

The total demand of all customers served by a single vehicle tour, computed as the sum of demands across all customers assigned to delivery sites visited on that tour, must not exceed the vehicle's capacity. In addition, each tour must form a single connected circuit through the depot rather than any collection of disconnected subloops among delivery sites.

The cumulative quantity delivered on a tour at any delivery site must be at least as large as the total demand of all customers that are jointly assigned to that site and served by that tour. The cumulative quantity at a delivery site on a given tour must not exceed the vehicle capacity multiplied by the number of route segments leaving that site on that tour; consequently, when a site is visited on a tour the cumulative quantity is bounded by the vehicle capacity, and when a site is not visited on a tour the cumulative quantity at that site is forced to zero.

A service-pairing quantity tracks, for each combination of customer, delivery site, and tour, whether that customer is assigned to that delivery site and that site is served by that tour. This quantity can be positive only if the site is actually served by that tour and only if the customer is actually assigned to that site. Conversely, whenever a customer is assigned to a site and that site is served by a tour, this quantity must be at least one. Each customer must be covered by exactly one such service pairing across all sites and all tours, which ensures that every customer is assigned to precisely one site on precisely one tour.

The goal is to minimize the sum of two cost components: the total vehicle routing cost, calculated as the sum of route-segment travel costs over all route segments used across all tours, and the total customer assignment cost, calculated as the sum of assignment costs for each customer-to-site pairing that is selected.
