# Problem Description

A logistics company must decide which facilities to open among a set of potential facility locations in order to serve a set of customers whose demands are uncertain. There are a given number of potential facilities and a given number of customers. Uncertainty in customer demands is captured through a finite set of scenarios, each occurring with a known probability (all scenarios are equally likely when the scenario set is generated by sampling). The company must commit to facility opening decisions before observing which demand scenario materializes; once a scenario is realized, the company allocates supply from open facilities to customers.

Each potential facility has a fixed opening cost and a maximum capacity. Each customer has a demand that varies across scenarios; the demand of each customer under each scenario is provided as input. For each facility-customer pair, a unit transportation cost is given, representing the cost of shipping one unit of product from that facility to that customer. The input data also includes the probability associated with each scenario.

The company must make two kinds of choices. First, for each potential facility, it must decide whether or not to open it. Second, for each scenario, it must determine how much of each customer's demand is served by each open facility. The facility opening decisions are made before the demand scenario is known, while the allocation decisions are made after the scenario is revealed.

Every customer's demand must be fully satisfied in every scenario: the total amount shipped to a customer from all facilities must be at least as large as that customer's demand under the given scenario. In each scenario, the total amount shipped from any facility across all customers may not exceed that facility's capacity if the facility is open, and must be zero if the facility is not open. Additionally, the total capacity of all opened facilities must be at least as large as the maximum total customer demand across all scenarios, ensuring that the company can always serve all customers regardless of which scenario occurs.

The goal is to minimize total expected cost, defined as the sum of the fixed opening costs of all opened facilities plus the expected (probability-weighted average over all scenarios) total transportation cost of shipping goods from facilities to customers.
