# Problem Description

A service company operates a fleet of identical vehicles, all stationed at a single depot node within an undirected network of nodes and edges. Each edge in the network has a traversal cost representing the expense of traveling along that edge without performing any service (called deadheading). A subset of the edges are designated as required edges, each carrying a positive demand quantity and a service cost incurred when a vehicle services that edge. The number of vehicles in the fleet is fixed and given.

The company must assign each required edge to exactly one vehicle for servicing and determine the route each vehicle follows through the network. A vehicle's route must start and end at the depot and form a valid closed walk that services all of its assigned edges. Every required edge must be serviced by exactly one vehicle across the entire fleet. A vehicle may traverse any edge (required or not) any number of times without servicing it, purely for the purpose of traveling between locations. A vehicle can only service a required edge that it physically reaches during its walk from the depot. Each vehicle has a capacity limit, and the sum of the demands of all required edges assigned to a single vehicle must not exceed that capacity.

The input data specifies the set of nodes and edges forming the undirected network, which edges are required, the demand on each required edge, the service cost for each required edge, the deadheading cost for each edge, the depot node, the number of vehicles, and the vehicle capacity. The goal is to minimize the total cost, computed as the sum of all service costs for the required edges serviced plus the sum of all deadheading costs incurred across all vehicles, where each deadheading traversal of an edge contributes its traversal cost once per crossing. Since every required edge is serviced exactly once, the total service cost component is constant across all feasible solutions, so the optimization effectively seeks to minimize total deadheading cost.
