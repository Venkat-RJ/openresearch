# Problem Description

A freight distribution system operates over two echelons. The system comprises a single depot, a set of satellites (intermediate transshipment facilities), and a set of customers. Each customer has a known demand and belongs to exactly one predefined group; the groups are disjoint and collectively cover all customers. The locations of the depot, satellites, and customers are given as coordinates in a plane, and the travel cost between any two locations equals the Euclidean distance between them.

In the first echelon, a fleet of identical large vehicles departs from the depot, visits a subset of the satellites to deliver freight, and returns to the depot. Each first-echelon vehicle has a carrying capacity, and the total freight delivered by a single first-echelon vehicle across all satellites it visits cannot exceed that capacity. The total number of first-echelon vehicles available is given. A first-echelon vehicle may visit multiple satellites in a single tour, but each satellite may be visited at most once by any given first-echelon vehicle. A satellite need not be visited at all if it is not needed. A first-echelon vehicle can only deliver freight to a satellite that it visits on its route, and the quantity delivered by any single vehicle to any single satellite cannot exceed that satellite's throughput capacity. Each satellite has a throughput capacity limiting the total quantity of freight it can handle across all first-echelon deliveries. Each satellite has a per-unit handling cost incurred on every unit of freight passing through it.

In the second echelon, a fleet of identical small vehicles operates from the satellites to serve customers. Each second-echelon vehicle departs from a satellite, visits one or more customers along a route, and returns to the same satellite. Every customer must be visited exactly once by exactly one second-echelon vehicle, meaning that for each customer the number of incoming trips equals the number of outgoing trips and both equal one. Each second-echelon vehicle has a carrying capacity, the total number of second-echelon vehicles available across the entire system is given, and each satellite has a limit on the maximum number of second-echelon vehicles that may depart from it.

Each customer is assigned to the satellite from which its serving vehicle departs. Each satellite is assigned to itself. If a second-echelon vehicle travels directly from one location to another, and the origin is assigned to a particular satellite, then the destination must also be assigned to that same satellite.

The grouping requirement mandates that all customers belonging to the same group must be assigned to the same satellite. For every customer in a group and every satellite, the customer's assignment to that satellite equals the group's assignment to that satellite. Different groups may be assigned to different satellites, or multiple groups may share a satellite, but a single group must not be split across satellites.

The quantity of freight delivered to each satellite by first-echelon vehicles must equal the total load departing that satellite on second-echelon trips. On each second-echelon trip between two locations, the load of goods must be consistent with customer demands: the total load arriving at a customer minus the total load departing from that customer equals that customer's demand. The load on any trip segment is zero if the segment is not traversed. When a segment is traversed, the load on it must be at least the demand of the destination customer and at most the second-echelon vehicle capacity minus the demand of the origin location, where the demand of a satellite is zero.

Each first-echelon vehicle's set of traversed arcs must form a single connected tour that begins and ends at the depot; disconnected cycles among satellites (subtours that do not pass through the depot) are not allowed.

The goal is to minimize the total cost, which is the sum of three components: the total travel cost of all first-echelon vehicle routes, the total handling cost at all satellites (proportional to the freight volume processed at each), and the total travel cost of all second-echelon vehicle routes.
