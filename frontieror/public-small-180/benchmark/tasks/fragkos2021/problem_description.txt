# Problem Description

A company must decide which facilities to open from a set of potential locations in order to serve a set of customers, where each facility is subject to random disruption that may render it temporarily unavailable. The number of potential facility locations and the number of customers are both given. Each customer has a known demand rate. Each potential facility has a known fixed setup cost to open. There is no limit on the amount of demand any single facility can serve. For every facility-customer pair, a unit shipment cost is given, representing the cost per unit of demand to transport goods from that facility to that customer. For each customer, a unit penalty cost is also given, representing the per-unit cost incurred when that customer cannot be served by any open, non-disrupted facility; the penalty cost for each customer is at least as large as the shipment cost from any facility to that customer.

Each facility, once opened, may fail due to disruptive events. The disruption status of all facilities is described by a joint probability distribution over all possible combinations of facility availability. In the stochastic setting, the full joint distribution over all disruption scenarios is exactly known, and the set of allowable distributions contains only that single distribution. In the more general distributionally robust setting, the exact joint distribution is not known but is constrained to lie within an ambiguity set defined by a collection of pieces of disruption information. Each piece of information specifies a subset of facilities required to be online and a subset required to be offline, together with a lower and upper bound on the probability that those conditions hold simultaneously. The true distribution must assign probabilities summing to one across all possible disruption scenarios and must satisfy every such probability interval constraint.

Given a set of opened facilities and a realized disruption outcome, each customer is served by the available facility (one that is both opened and not disrupted) offering the lowest unit shipment cost for that customer. A dummy always-available penalty option exists so that if all of a customer's opened facilities are disrupted, the customer incurs the penalty cost instead. The total transportation and penalty cost under a specific disruption realization is therefore the sum over all customers of that customer's demand multiplied by the minimum unit cost among all opened and non-disrupted facilities serving that customer, including the penalty option.

The company must choose which facilities to open before disruptions are realized. The goal is to minimize the sum of two cost components: the total fixed setup cost of all opened facilities, plus the worst-case expected transportation and penalty cost, where the worst case is taken over all disruption probability distributions consistent with the ambiguity set. In the stochastic special case where the distribution is exactly known, this worst-case expectation reduces to the ordinary expected transportation and penalty cost under the given distribution.
