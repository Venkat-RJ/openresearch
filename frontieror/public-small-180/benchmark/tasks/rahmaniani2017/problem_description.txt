# Problem Description

A logistics network routes commodity shipments between origin-destination pairs through a hub-and-spoke system. The network consists of a set of nodes, a subset of which are candidates for hub facilities. Every pair of nodes with a positive shipment volume defines a commodity, characterized by its origin node, its destination node, and the amount of flow to be shipped. Each candidate hub node has a known fixed setup cost for opening a hub at that location. Pairwise distances between all nodes are given; these distances are symmetric and satisfy the triangle inequality.

Every commodity must be routed from its origin to its destination through one or two open hubs. Specifically, each shipment travels from its origin to a first hub, optionally transfers to a second hub, and then continues from that second hub to its destination. The cost of shipping a commodity along a path consisting of origin, first hub, second hub, and destination equals the commodity's flow volume multiplied by the sum of three leg costs: the collection leg cost (the distance from origin to the first hub scaled by a collection cost parameter), the transfer leg cost (the distance between the first and second hubs scaled by an interhub discount factor), and the distribution leg cost (the distance from the second hub to the destination scaled by a distribution cost parameter). When a commodity is routed through a single hub, the first and second hubs coincide and the transfer leg has zero length. The interhub discount factor is strictly between zero and one and is strictly less than both the collection and distribution cost parameters, reflecting economies of scale on interhub links. In the symmetric case studied here, the collection and distribution cost parameters are both equal to one.

The planner must decide which candidate nodes to open as hubs and, for each commodity, which ordered pair (first hub, second hub) of open hubs to use for routing (the two hubs may be the same node, meaning the commodity is routed through a single hub). There is no limit on the number of hubs that may be opened, no capacity restriction on any hub or link, and each commodity may be assigned to any such hub pair so long as both hubs in the pair are open. A commodity whose origin and destination are the same node is always routed through exactly one hub.

Each commodity must be assigned to exactly one ordered hub pair. If a commodity is assigned to a hub pair, both hubs in that pair must be open. The goal is to minimize the total cost, defined as the sum of all fixed hub setup costs for the opened hubs plus the total transportation cost across all commodities, where each commodity's transportation cost is determined by the hub pair it is assigned to as described above.

Observation on symmetry. Because the distances are symmetric and the collection and distribution cost parameters are equal, each commodity's routing cost through the ordered pair (i, j) equals its cost through the reverse ordered pair (j, i). Consequently, without loss of optimality, routing decisions for each commodity may be enumerated over unordered hub pairs rather than ordered ones. This observation is a property of symmetric instances, not part of the problem definition itself.
