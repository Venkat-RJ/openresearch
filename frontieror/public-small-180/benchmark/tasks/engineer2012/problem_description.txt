# Problem Description

A shipping company distributes a single bulk product from a set of supply ports to a set of demand ports using a fleet of heterogeneous vessels over a discrete planning horizon of T time periods. Each supply port produces a known amount of product each period, and each demand port consumes a known amount each period; these production and consumption rates may vary from period to period. Every port has an initial inventory at the start of the horizon and a storage capacity that may also vary by period; inventory at any port must never fall below zero or exceed its storage capacity in any period. Each port also has a minimum and maximum quantity that can be loaded or unloaded there in a single time period; if a vessel performs a load or discharge operation at a port in a given period, the quantity transferred must fall between these bounds.

Each vessel has a cargo capacity, and the amount of product on board a vessel at any time must never exceed this capacity. Additionally, each vessel-port combination has a draft limit specifying the maximum cargo a vessel may carry when entering that port. Each vessel has an earliest availability time and a latest availability time defining the window during which it may operate, and the vessel begins with a specified initial load on board. Travel between any pair of ports takes a known number of time periods that depends on the origin and destination. A vessel that remains idle at a port for one period incurs a demurrage cost per day, and each day a vessel is in transit incurs a transportation cost per day; both costs are vessel-specific. At supply ports, procuring product costs a per-unit amount specific to that port, and at demand ports, delivering product earns a per-unit revenue specific to that port.

Over the planning horizon, the company must determine, for each vessel, the sequence of ports it visits, the timing of each visit, and the quantity of product loaded or discharged at each visited port and time period. At a supply port a vessel loads product, removing it from port inventory, and at a demand port a vessel discharges product, adding it to port inventory. Inventory at each port evolves over time: at a supply port, the inventory at the end of a period equals the inventory at the end of the previous period plus that period's production minus the total amount loaded onto all vessels at that port during that period; at a demand port, the inventory at the end of a period equals the inventory at the end of the previous period minus that period's consumption plus the total amount discharged by all vessels at that port during that period. Each vessel follows a route from its earliest availability time and location to its latest availability time and location, where each leg of the route represents either a sailing from one port to a different port (consuming the appropriate travel time) or remaining idle at the same port for one additional period. At each port and time along its route, the vessel may choose whether to load or discharge, and if so, how much, subject to the per-port minimum and maximum transfer bounds. The cumulative cargo aboard a vessel as it progresses along its route must remain between zero and the applicable upper bound at each port visited, where that upper bound reflects both the vessel's cargo capacity and any draft limit at the port being entered. For each vessel, exactly one complete voyage plan (route, timing, and load/discharge schedule) must be selected, and the load-or-discharge decision at each port and time period for each vessel must be an all-or-nothing choice: either the vessel performs a transfer operation at that location and time, or it does not.

The goal is to minimize total cost, defined as the sum across all vessels of each vessel's voyage cost. A voyage's cost comprises the transportation costs for all sailing legs, the demurrage costs for all idle periods at ports, and the net expense from product handling, which equals the procurement cost paid at supply ports for each unit loaded minus the revenue earned at demand ports for each unit discharged.
