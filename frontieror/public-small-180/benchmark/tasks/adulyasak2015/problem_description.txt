# Problem Description

A production plant manufactures a single product and distributes it to a set of customers over a discrete, finite planning horizon of multiple time periods. A fleet of identical capacitated vehicles, each with the same load capacity, is available at the plant to make deliveries. The plant and each customer have a known initial inventory at the start of the horizon, a per-unit inventory holding cost, and a maximum inventory capacity. The plant has a maximum production capacity per period, a unit production cost, and a fixed setup cost that is incurred in any period in which production takes place. Each customer has a known demand in each period (which may be uncertain and described by a set of scenarios with known probabilities) and a per-unit penalty cost for demand that is left unmet. The transportation cost of moving a vehicle directly between any two locations (plant or customer) is known and symmetric.

In each period, the company decides whether to set up production at the plant, how much to produce, which customers each vehicle will visit, the delivery quantity from each vehicle to each visited customer, the route each dispatched vehicle follows starting and ending at the plant, and the resulting end-of-period inventory at the plant and at each customer. A customer may be visited by at most one vehicle per period. Production may occur in a period only if a setup has been performed in that period, and the production quantity cannot exceed the plant's production capacity. The plant's inventory balance requires that the inventory carried from the previous period plus the quantity produced equals the total quantity shipped to customers plus the inventory remaining at the plant; the remaining inventory cannot exceed the plant's storage capacity. Each customer's inventory balance requires that the inventory carried from the previous period plus the total quantity delivered plus any unmet demand equals the demand in that period plus the remaining inventory, and the ending inventory plus the period's demand cannot exceed the customer's storage capacity (deliveries arrive before demand is consumed).

The total quantity loaded onto any vehicle in a period cannot exceed the vehicle capacity, and a vehicle can deliver to a customer only if it visits that customer on its route in that period. The delivery quantity to a customer from a vehicle is bounded by the customer's remaining storage capacity and by the customer's future demand from that period onward. Each vehicle route must start and end at the plant and form a single tour through its assigned customers.

When demands are uncertain, the setup, visit, and routing decisions must be fixed before demand realizations are observed, while the production, delivery, inventory, and unmet-demand quantities may adapt to the realized demand information available up to that period. The objective is to minimize the total expected cost over the planning horizon: the fixed setup costs, the transportation costs of all routes operated, the unit production costs, the inventory holding costs at the plant and at all customers, and the penalty costs for unmet demand, with scenario-dependent cost components weighted by their probabilities.
