# Problem Description

A team of defending robots and a team of attacking robots operate on a two-dimensional playing field with a regular-polygon Defense Zone centered at the origin. Its M_dz side half-spaces have apothem R_dz. There are N_D defenders and N_A attackers. Each attacker moves in a straight line toward the Defense Zone at a constant velocity. Each defender is governed by linear discrete-time dynamics and is steered by choosing control inputs at each time step. The objective is to find a control-input sequence for every defender that minimizes the number of time steps during which attackers occupy the Defense Zone, with an optional small penalty on the defenders' cumulative control effort.

Time is discretized. Defenders take N_u control steps at a uniform interval T_u, and attackers are tracked at N_a sample steps at a uniform interval T_a. Each defender has a two-dimensional position and two-dimensional velocity. Between consecutive control steps the defender's state evolves linearly according to the following first-order dynamics: the velocity decays toward the applied control input exponentially with time constant one, and the position is the integral of that velocity. Concretely, the next-step velocity in each coordinate equals the current velocity times exp(−T_u) plus the control input times (1 − exp(−T_u)), and the next-step position in each coordinate equals the current position plus the current velocity times (1 − exp(−T_u)) plus the control input times (T_u − 1 + exp(−T_u)). Each defender's initial position and velocity are given.

Each defender's control input at every step is a two-component vector lying in the M_u-side regular polygon inscribed in the unit disk, using half-space right-hand side cos(pi/M_u). Each attacker moves at constant velocity while active: its position at the next sample step equals its current position plus its velocity times T_a. An attacker becomes inactive (and stops moving thereafter) as soon as it either enters the Defense Zone or is intercepted by at least one defender.

An attacker is considered inside the Defense Zone at a given step if its position satisfies every one of the M_dz polygon half-spaces. An attacker is considered intercepted by a defender at the attacker's sample time if its relative position satisfies every one of the M_I regular-polygon half-spaces with apothem R_I, centered at the defender's interpolated position (linearly interpolated from the surrounding control-step states and inputs).

Defender avoidance is enforced at the N_o=N_u positive control-step boundaries, as used by the released MILP. At every such boundary, each defender must lie outside at least one half-space of the four-side obstacle polygon centered at the origin with apothem R_dz.

The objective to minimize is the total number of attacker-time-step pairs during which the attacker occupies the Defense Zone (summed over all attackers and all sample steps from 1 through N_a), plus a non-negative weight epsilon times the total control effort (summed over defenders, control steps, and control coordinates, with effort measured as the sum of absolute values of control components). The weight epsilon is zero when only zone penetration is penalized, or a small positive number (such as 0.1) when fuel conservation is an additional secondary goal.

The input data for an instance specifies: the number of defenders N_D and attackers N_A; the polygon apothems R_dz and R_I and side counts M_u, M_I, and M_dz; the discretization step counts N_u and N_a (with obstacle checks defined as N_o=N_u); the uniform time steps T_u and T_a; the cost weight epsilon; for each defender its initial position and velocity in two dimensions; and for each attacker its initial position and constant velocity vector in two dimensions.
