# Problem Description

A supplier ships a single product to a set of n retailers over a discrete time horizon of H periods. At each period, a known quantity of product is produced or made available at the supplier, and each retailer consumes a known quantity of product. The supplier begins with a given starting inventory, and each retailer has a given starting inventory that does not exceed its maximum inventory level. Each retailer has a maximum inventory level, and the supplier holds product at a per-unit inventory cost per period, while each retailer incurs its own per-unit inventory cost per period. A single vehicle with a fixed capacity is available to deliver product from the supplier to any subset of retailers in each period, traveling a route that starts and ends at the supplier. The symmetric transportation cost between every pair of locations (supplier and retailers) is given.

The supplier must decide, for each period, which retailers to visit and how much product to ship to each, as well as the route the vehicle follows to serve the visited retailers. Whenever a retailer is visited in a given period, the quantity shipped must exactly equal the difference between the retailer's maximum inventory level and its current inventory level at that time, thereby raising the retailer's inventory to its maximum. If a retailer is not visited in a period, it receives nothing. The total quantity loaded onto the vehicle in any period must not exceed the vehicle's capacity.

The inventory at the supplier at each period equals the supplier's inventory in the previous period, plus the quantity produced at the supplier in the previous period, minus the total quantity shipped to all retailers in the previous period. In each period of the planning horizon, the supplier's inventory must be at least the total quantity shipped to all retailers in that period. The inventory at each retailer at each period equals the retailer's inventory in the previous period, plus the quantity received from the supplier in the previous period, minus the quantity consumed by the retailer in the previous period. No retailer's inventory may ever fall below zero at any period from one through the end of the extended horizon, which includes one period beyond the last delivery period. Inventory costs at both the supplier and all retailers are assessed over this extended horizon.

In each period, if any retailer receives a delivery, the vehicle route must include the supplier. The route in each period forms a single tour: the total number of route-segment traversals touching each visited location (supplier and retailers) equals exactly two in that period. The route must not contain any loop among retailers that does not pass through the supplier. Specifically, for any subset of retailers, the number of route segments connecting only pairs of locations within that subset cannot exceed the number of visited retailers in that subset minus the visit status of one chosen retailer from that subset.

The route segments in each period between two retailers are either used or not used. The route segment between any retailer and the supplier may be traversed zero, one, or two times in a period, the value of two corresponding to a direct round-trip serving a single retailer. A retailer can only appear in a route segment if that retailer is visited in that period, and the supplier-to-retailer segment can only be traversed twice if that retailer is visited.

The goal is to minimize the total cost, which is the sum of three components: the supplier's inventory holding cost summed over all periods of the extended horizon, the retailers' inventory holding costs summed over all retailers and all periods of the extended horizon, and the transportation costs summed over all route segments used across all delivery periods.
