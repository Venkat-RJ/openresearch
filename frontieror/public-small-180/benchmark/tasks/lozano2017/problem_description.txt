# Problem Description

A bilevel mixed-integer linear program involves two sequential decision makers, called the leader and the follower, who each control a separate set of variables and pursue separate goals, but whose choices are coupled through shared conditions. The leader acts first by selecting values for its own set of variables, after which the follower observes the leader's choices and responds by selecting values for a second set of variables that maximize the follower's own goal, subject to conditions that depend on both players' variables. The leader must anticipate this rational response when making its decision. The total number of variables is split equally between the two players: the leader controls n1 variables and the follower controls n2 variables, where n1 equals n2. All leader variables take nonnegative integer values. A designated subset of the follower's variables, comprising half of them, must also take nonnegative integer values; the remaining follower variables take nonnegative real values. The specific indices identifying which follower variables are integer-restricted are given as part of the input.

The input data specifies two sets of linear inequalities. The first set, called the upper-level conditions, consists of m1 inequalities, where m1 equals the integer part of four-tenths of n1. Each such inequality involves both leader and follower variables: a row of coefficients applied to the leader variables, a row of coefficients applied to the follower variables, and a right-hand side value. The second set, called the lower-level conditions, consists of m2 inequalities with the same structure, where m2 equals m1. The coefficient matrices for both levels have nonnegative integer entries drawn from zero to ten. The right-hand side values of the upper-level conditions are positive integers drawn from thirty to one hundred thirty, and the right-hand side values of the lower-level conditions are positive integers drawn from ten to one hundred ten.

The leader's objective is to maximize a linear function of the leader's own variables. In this released benchmark profile, the coefficients d1 applied to follower variables in the general leader objective are explicitly fixed to zero; each instance records this convention with `leader_follower_objective_coefficients_fixed_zero=true`. The coefficients applied to the leader's variables are integers ranging from negative fifty to positive fifty. The follower's objective is to maximize a different linear function of the follower's variables only, with coefficients drawn as integers from negative fifty to positive fifty. The follower's objective does not depend on the leader's variables. Entries of `integer_follower_vars` use one-based indices.

The interaction between the two players proceeds as follows. The leader selects values for its variables. Both the upper-level and lower-level conditions must be satisfied jointly by the leader's and follower's variable values. Given the leader's fixed choice, the follower selects variable values that maximize the follower's objective among all follower solutions satisfying the lower-level conditions (with the leader's variables treated as fixed parameters in those conditions). The leader must choose its variables so that, after the follower responds optimally, the upper-level conditions are also satisfied. Under the optimistic assumption, when the follower has multiple optimal solutions yielding the same follower objective value, the follower selects the one that is most favorable to the leader.

The goal is to find the leader's variable values and the corresponding follower response that maximize the leader's objective, subject to all upper-level and lower-level conditions being satisfied and subject to the requirement that the follower's response is optimal for the follower's own problem given the leader's choice.
