# Problem Description

A delivery company operates a single truck equipped with a fleet of heterogeneous drones to serve a set of customers from a single depot. Each drone has its own speed parameter and battery capacity, meaning faster drones consume more energy per unit distance and consequently have shorter maximum flight durations. The truck departs from the depot, visits a subset of customer locations along a route, and returns to the depot. At any node the truck visits, one or more drones may be dispatched simultaneously to serve nearby customers. A drone delivery from a given node consists of a round trip: the drone flies from the truck's current location to the assigned customer, delivers the demand, and returns to the same node. Each drone carries exactly one customer's demand per trip but may make multiple such round trips from the same or different nodes over the course of the route, provided its total battery consumption across all assigned deliveries does not exceed its battery capacity. While drones are out making deliveries, the truck must wait at the dispatch node until every drone dispatched from that node has returned; only then may the truck proceed to its next stop.

The input data specifies the number of customers, the number of drones, the two-dimensional coordinates of the depot and all customers, the truck travel time for every ordered pair of nodes (which includes the service time at the destination node), and for each drone and every ordered pair of a potential launch node and a customer, both the total battery consumption and the total elapsed time for the round-trip delivery. Each drone's battery capacity is also given.

The company must determine the truck's route through a subset of customer locations starting and ending at the depot, which customers are served directly by the truck along its route versus served by a drone, and for each drone-served customer, from which node the drone is launched and which specific drone performs the delivery. The truck's waiting time at each visited node must also be determined.

Every customer must be served exactly once, either by the truck visiting that customer directly on its route or by exactly one drone making a delivery to that customer from some node on the truck's route. Drones may only be dispatched from nodes that the truck actually visits. The truck's route must form a single connected tour from the depot through the truck-visited customers and back to the depot, and no customer appears more than once on the truck's route (customers served by drones are not visited by the truck at all). The total battery consumed by each drone across all of its assigned deliveries must not exceed that drone's battery capacity. The truck's waiting time at each node must be at least as large as the combined round-trip delivery time of each drone for all deliveries that drone performs from that node. Because this must hold for every drone independently, the truck effectively waits for whichever drone has the longest total delivery time from that node before departing.

The goal is to minimize the total completion time, defined as the sum of the truck's travel times along all arcs of its route plus the sum of the truck's waiting times at all nodes it visits.
