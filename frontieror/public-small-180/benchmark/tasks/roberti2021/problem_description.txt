# Problem Description

A single truck, equipped with a single drone, is located at a depot and must serve a set of at least two customers. The input data specifies the number of customers, the location of the depot and each customer in a two-dimensional plane, a truck travel time for every ordered pair of locations (including the depot), and a drone travel time for every ordered pair of locations. Truck travel times follow the taxicab (Manhattan) metric and drone travel times follow the Euclidean metric scaled by a speed factor, so the two time matrices are generally different, with the drone typically being faster. The network is represented as a directed graph whose nodes comprise the depot (appearing as both an origin node and a distinct destination node) and all customers. Arcs exist from the depot origin to each customer, between every ordered pair of distinct customers, and from each customer to the depot destination. No arc connects the depot origin directly to the depot destination, and no arcs lead from the depot destination to any other node or from any node back to the depot origin.

Each customer must be served exactly once, and its service is classified into one of three types: it is served by the truck alone, by the drone alone, or by both the truck and the drone arriving together (a combined visit). The truck must depart from the depot origin exactly once and return to the depot destination exactly once, traversing a sequence of arcs through customer nodes. The drone must likewise depart from the depot origin exactly once and return to the depot destination exactly once, traversing its own sequence of arcs. At every customer node, the number of truck arcs entering that node equals the number leaving it, and independently the number of drone arcs entering that node equals the number leaving it. For the truck, the total number of arcs leaving a customer equals one if that customer is either a truck-only customer or a combined customer, and zero otherwise. Symmetrically, the total number of drone arcs leaving a customer equals one if that customer is either a drone-only customer or a combined customer, and zero otherwise.

Each node is assigned an arrival time that is at least zero. If the truck uses an arc from one node to another, the arrival time at the arc's end node must be at least the arrival time at the arc's start node plus the truck travel time on that arc. If the drone uses an arc from one node to another, the arrival time at the arc's end node must be at least the arrival time at the arc's start node plus the drone travel time on that arc. At a combined customer, where both vehicles visit, the arrival time therefore reflects whichever vehicle arrives later. These timing requirements also prevent either vehicle from forming a closed loop that bypasses the depot, because arrival times must strictly increase along each vehicle's path.

For any pair of distinct customers, the number of drone arcs connecting them, counting both directions, cannot exceed the number of those two customers that have combined status. This means that if the drone uses an arc between two customers in one direction, at least one of them must be a combined customer, and if the drone uses arcs between two customers in both directions, then both must be combined customers. Additionally, the drone cannot depart from the depot origin to serve a single customer and return directly to the depot destination on the same trip; that is, for any customer, the drone cannot simultaneously use the arc from the depot origin to that customer and the arc from that customer to the depot destination.

The total tour duration equals the arrival time at the depot destination node. The goal is to determine the assignment of each customer to truck-only, drone-only, or combined status, the routing of the truck and the drone through their respective sequences of customers, and the arrival time at each node, so as to minimize the total tour completion time.
