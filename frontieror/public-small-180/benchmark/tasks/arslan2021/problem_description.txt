# Problem Description

A company manages a collection of items, each characterized by a weight, a nominal profit, a maximum profit degradation, an outsourcing penalty cost, and a repair capacity requirement. The company has a knapsack with a fixed total capacity. In a first planning phase, before any uncertainty is revealed, the company must decide which items to select for potential in-house production. This benchmark then uses the LP-relaxed recourse approximation of the binary model: after an adversarial degradation is revealed, the fractions produced as-is and repaired may take any value between zero and one. Items not selected in the first phase cannot be produced or repaired. This relaxation is the problem evaluated by the supplied model, checker, and reference objectives; it should not be interpreted as the original binary-recourse Eq. (43).

The uncertainty in profit degradation is governed by a vector of degradation factors, one per item, each ranging from zero to one. The total sum of all degradation factors across items is limited by an uncertainty budget. The actual profit of an item produced as-is equals its nominal profit minus the product of its maximum degradation and the corresponding degradation factor. An item that is repaired recovers its full nominal profit regardless of the degradation factor. An item that is outsourced yields its nominal profit minus its outsourcing penalty, regardless of degradation. The adversary chooses the degradation factors to inflict the worst possible outcome on the company, subject to the budget and per-item bounds.

The input data specifies the number of items, and for each item: its weight (consumption of knapsack capacity when produced), its nominal profit, its maximum profit degradation (the worst-case profit reduction when the degradation factor equals one), its outsourcing penalty cost, and the additional capacity consumed when that item is repaired instead of produced as-is. The data also specifies the total knapsack capacity and the uncertainty budget controlling the total allowable degradation across all items.

The company must choose which items to select in the first phase. The second-stage production and repair variables are continuous fractions in [0,1]. A repaired fraction cannot exceed the produced fraction. The total capacity consumed by production and repair together must not exceed the knapsack capacity: each produced fraction consumes its proportional item weight, and each repaired fraction consumes its proportional additional repair capacity. Only items selected in the first phase may receive a positive production or repair fraction.

The goal is to minimize the total worst-case cost. This cost is the sum of two components. The first component, determined before uncertainty is revealed, equals the sum over all selected items of the difference between each item's outsourcing penalty and its nominal profit. The second component, determined after the adversary reveals the degradation, equals the sum over all produced items of that item's maximum degradation multiplied by its degradation factor minus that item's outsourcing penalty, minus the sum over all repaired items of that item's maximum degradation multiplied by its degradation factor. Equivalently, selecting an item incurs a baseline cost of outsourcing penalty minus nominal profit; producing that item as-is then offsets the outsourcing penalty but exposes it to degradation; repairing it further offsets the degradation exposure. The adversary maximizes this second-stage cost over the uncertainty set, and the company minimizes against the adversary's worst case.
