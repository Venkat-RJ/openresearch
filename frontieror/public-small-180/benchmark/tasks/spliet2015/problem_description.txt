# Problem Description

A distribution company operates a fleet of identical vehicles, each with a known cargo capacity, to deliver goods from a central depot to a set of customers. The depot has a starting location and an ending location (vehicles depart from and return to the depot), and there are a known number of customers, each at a fixed geographic position. Travel costs and travel times between every pair of locations (including the depot endpoints and all customers) are given and satisfy the triangle inequality.

Customer demand is uncertain at the time delivery time windows must be set. Uncertainty is represented by a finite set of demand scenarios, each with a known probability of occurrence and a specific demand quantity for every customer. Every customer's demand in every scenario is strictly positive and does not exceed the vehicle capacity. Depot demand is zero in all scenarios.

Each customer has an exogenous time window, a broad interval during which deliveries are permitted (for example, imposed by local regulations or store operating hours). The depot also has an exogenous time window governing when vehicles may depart and return. In addition, each customer has a prescribed endogenous time window width, which is assumed not to exceed the length of that customer's exogenous time window. The company must assign to each customer an endogenous time window whose width equals this prescribed value. The start of each customer's endogenous time window may be placed anywhere such that the entire endogenous window falls within the customer's exogenous time window; that is, the start time must be no earlier than the beginning of the exogenous window and no later than the end of the exogenous window minus the endogenous width.

The endogenous time window assignments are made before demand is revealed and are fixed across all scenarios. After demand becomes known under a particular scenario, the company must design vehicle routes for that scenario. A route is a path beginning at the starting depot, visiting a sequence of customers, and ending at the ending depot. A route is valid for a given scenario if the total demand of customers on the route does not exceed the vehicle capacity, service at every location on the route occurs within that location's exogenous time window, and for any two consecutively visited locations the service time at the second is no earlier than the service time at the first plus the travel time between them (vehicles may wait at a location if they arrive before the exogenous window opens). An unlimited number of vehicles is available.

The company must choose two things simultaneously: the endogenous time window start time for every customer (a single assignment used across all scenarios), and for each scenario a collection of valid routes. Every customer must be visited exactly once in every scenario. In every scenario, the time at which each customer is served must fall within that customer's assigned endogenous time window; specifically, the service time must be no earlier than the endogenous window start and no later than the endogenous window start plus the endogenous width.

The goal is to minimize the expected total travel cost, computed as the probability-weighted sum over all scenarios of the total route costs used in each scenario, where the cost of a route is the sum of the travel costs along its arcs.
