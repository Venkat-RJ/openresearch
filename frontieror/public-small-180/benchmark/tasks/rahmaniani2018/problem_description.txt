# Problem Description

An electricity system planner must determine the least-cost strategy for expanding, retiring, and operating generation, storage, and transmission resources across a network of spatial zones over a single planning year. The system contains a set of resource clusters (generators, storage units, and hydropower plants), each located in a specific zone, connected by a set of interregional transmission lines. The planning year is divided into weekly subperiods, each containing 168 hourly time steps. Each subperiod carries a weight reflecting how many weeks of the year it represents, and each hour within a subperiod receives a weight equal to the subperiod weight divided by 168. Resources fall into several overlapping categories: those subject to unit commitment (thermal plants whose individual units must be started up and shut down), storage resources (batteries), hydropower resources (reservoir-based hydro), resources qualifying for a renewable portfolio standard, and resources that cannot be retired.

The input data specifies, for every resource cluster: existing installed power capacity in MW, existing energy storage capacity in MWh (for storage resources), the capacity size per unit in MW (relevant for unit commitment resources; set to one for all others), maximum allowable new capacity in MW, investment cost per MW-year, fixed operations and maintenance cost per MW-year, time-varying variable operating cost per MWh, CO2 emission factor in tons per MWh, a time-varying availability fraction between zero and one giving the maximum fraction of installed capacity dispatchable in each hour, a minimum output fraction, maximum ramp-up and ramp-down rates as fractions of capacity per hour, minimum up-time and minimum down-time in hours (for unit commitment resources), and startup cost per unit (for unit commitment resources). For storage resources the data additionally specifies existing and maximum energy capacity in MWh, energy capacity size per unit in MWh (set to one for all resources not subject to unit commitment), energy investment cost per MWh-year, energy fixed O&M cost per MWh-year, charging efficiency, discharging efficiency, self-discharge rate per hour, and minimum and maximum duration ratios (MWh per MW) linking energy capacity to power capacity. For hydropower resources the data specifies a fixed duration ratio (MWh per MW) and an hourly normalized inflow profile as a fraction of power capacity. For each transmission line the data gives existing capacity in MW, maximum new capacity in MW, and investment cost per MW-year. For each zone and each hour the data gives net electricity demand in MWh. Multiple non-served-energy segments are defined, each with a cost per MWh that may vary by zone and a maximum fraction of zonal demand that may go unserved. A policy scenario specifies whether a renewable portfolio standard is active (with a required share of total demand and a noncompliance penalty cost per MWh) and whether a CO2 emissions cap is active (with a cap defined as five percent of total weighted demand, in tons, and a noncompliance penalty cost per ton).

The planner must decide, for each resource cluster, how many new units of power capacity to build and how many existing units to retire (in whole-unit increments of the cluster's capacity size), and likewise for storage energy capacity. For each transmission line the planner must choose the new capacity to add; this expansion capacity variable is modeled as an integer-valued number of MW in the MILP formulation. These investment and retirement decisions determine the total installed power capacity, energy capacity, and transmission capacity for each resource and line. Operationally, for every resource and every hour, the planner must decide the generation (injection) in MWh, and for storage resources also the withdrawal (charging) in MWh. For each transmission line and hour the planner must set the power flow in MWh, which may be positive or negative to represent flow in either direction. For each non-served-energy segment, zone, and hour the planner must set the amount of curtailed demand. For unit commitment resources the planner must determine, for each hour, the number of committed units, the number of units started up, and the number shut down. For storage resources the planner must track the state of charge in MWh at each hour. For hydropower resources the planner must track the reservoir level in MWh and any spillage in MWh at each hour. For each subperiod the planner must also set noncompliance slack amounts for the renewable portfolio standard and the CO2 cap, if those policies are active.

The total installed power capacity for each resource equals its existing capacity plus the capacity size times the difference between newly built units and retired units. The product of the capacity size and the number of new units built must not exceed the maximum allowable new capacity, and the product of the capacity size and the number of retired units must not exceed the existing capacity. Resources designated as non-retirable may not be retired. The same structure applies to storage energy capacity: the total energy capacity equals existing energy capacity plus the energy capacity size times the difference between new and retired energy units, with analogous upper bounds on new builds and retirements. Additionally, the energy capacity of a storage resource must lie between the minimum duration ratio times the power capacity and the maximum duration ratio times the power capacity. For transmission lines, new capacity must not exceed the maximum allowable expansion, and total line capacity equals existing capacity plus new capacity.

In every zone and every hour, the power balance must hold: the sum of generation from all resources in that zone, minus withdrawals by storage resources in that zone, minus flows on lines leaving that zone, plus flows on lines entering that zone, plus all non-served energy in that zone, must equal the zone's demand. For resources not subject to unit commitment, generation in any hour must not exceed the availability fraction times the installed power capacity. For storage resources, withdrawals must not exceed the availability fraction times the power capacity, the sum of generation and withdrawal must not exceed the power capacity, charging (the product of charging efficiency and withdrawal) must not exceed the remaining room in the battery (energy capacity minus current state of charge), and generation divided by discharging efficiency must not exceed the current state of charge. The state of charge must not exceed the energy capacity. For hydropower resources the reservoir level must not exceed the duration ratio times the power capacity.

For resources not subject to unit commitment, storage, or hydro classification, generation in each hour must be at least the minimum output fraction times the installed power capacity. For hydropower resources, generation plus spillage must be at least the minimum output fraction times the installed power capacity. The flow on each transmission line in each hour must have absolute value no greater than the line's total capacity. Non-served energy in each segment, zone, and hour must not exceed that segment's maximum fraction of the zone's demand in that hour.

Within each subperiod, storage state of charge evolves from one hour to the next: the change in state of charge equals the charging efficiency times the withdrawal, minus the generation divided by the discharging efficiency, minus the self-discharge rate times the previous hour's state of charge. The first and last hours of each subperiod are linked circularly, so that the state of charge wraps around within each week. Hydropower reservoir levels follow an analogous balance: the change in reservoir level equals the normalized inflow fraction times the power capacity, minus generation, minus spillage, with the same circular linkage between the first and last hours of each subperiod.

For resources not subject to unit commitment, the hour-to-hour increase in generation must not exceed the ramp-up rate times the installed power capacity, and the hour-to-hour decrease must not exceed the ramp-down rate times the installed capacity. These ramp limits also apply at the subperiod boundary using circular indexing, so the change from the last hour to the first hour of each subperiod is likewise bounded.

For unit commitment resources, the number of committed units times the capacity size must not exceed the installed power capacity, and the same bound applies individually to the number of started-up units and the number of shut-down units. Generation from a unit commitment resource must be at least the number of committed units times the minimum output fraction times the capacity size, and at most the number of committed units times the availability fraction times the capacity size. The change in committed units from one hour to the next equals the number of startups minus shutdowns in that hour, with circular linkage at subperiod boundaries. Ramp limits for unit commitment resources are tighter: the increase in generation is bounded by the capacity size times the ramp-up rate times the number of units that were committed but not just started, plus the capacity size times the lesser of the availability fraction and the larger of the minimum output fraction and the ramp-up rate, applied to the number of units just started, minus the capacity size times the minimum output fraction times the number of units just shut down. The decrease in generation is bounded by the capacity size times the ramp-down rate times the number of units that were committed but not just started, plus the capacity size times the lesser of the availability fraction and the larger of the minimum output fraction and the ramp-down rate, applied to the number of units just shut down, minus the capacity size times the minimum output fraction times the number of units just started. These ramp limits also apply at the circular subperiod boundaries. A committed unit must remain online for at least the minimum up-time: the number of committed units at any hour must be at least the sum of startups over the preceding hours equal in number to the minimum up-time, using circular indexing within the subperiod. Similarly, the number of committed units plus the number of shutdowns over the preceding hours equal in number to the minimum down-time must not exceed the total number of available units (installed capacity divided by capacity size).

When the renewable portfolio standard policy is active, the sum over all subperiods of the weighted generation from qualifying resources plus the noncompliance slack must be at least the required share times total weighted demand across all zones and subperiods. When the CO2 cap policy is active, the sum over all subperiods of the weighted CO2 emissions from all generation and storage withdrawals minus the noncompliance slack must not exceed the emissions cap, which is five percent of total weighted demand.

The goal is to minimize the total annual system cost. This cost is the sum of six components: fixed costs, which include generation investment cost (investment cost per MW-year times capacity size times new units built, for all resources, plus a parallel term for hydro resources using the energy investment cost times the duration ratio times new units built), storage energy investment cost (energy investment cost per MWh-year times energy capacity size times new energy units built), transmission investment cost (investment cost per MW-year times new line capacity), generation fixed O&M (fixed O&M cost per MW-year times total power capacity, with a parallel hydro term using the energy fixed O&M cost times the duration ratio times power capacity), and storage energy fixed O&M (energy fixed O&M cost per MWh-year times total energy capacity); variable operating costs, which are the sum over all hours and resources of the hourly weight times the variable cost per MWh times generation, plus the same for storage withdrawals; non-served energy costs, which are the sum over all hours, zones, and segments of the hourly weight times the segment-and-zone cost per MWh times the curtailed demand; startup costs, which are the sum over all hours and unit commitment resources of the hourly weight times the startup cost times the number of startups; renewable portfolio standard noncompliance cost, the penalty cost per MWh times the noncompliance slack summed over all subperiods; and CO2 cap noncompliance cost, the penalty cost per ton times the noncompliance slack summed over all subperiods.
