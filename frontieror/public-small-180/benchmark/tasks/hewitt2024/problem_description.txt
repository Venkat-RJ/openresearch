# Problem Description

A company sources and sells multiple products, each of which can be manufactured at one or more capacitated production facilities. The input data specifies a set of products and a set of facilities. Each facility has a maximum total volume capacity that limits the combined allocation of raw materials across all products at that facility. For each product-facility pair, there is a per-unit manufacturing cost representing the cost of allocating raw materials (quoted in finished-goods units) to that facility. Each product has a full sale price at which finished goods can be sold when demand exists, and a discounted price at which excess inventory beyond demand can be sold. The full sale price is always strictly greater than the discounted price, and the discounted price is always strictly less than the manufacturing cost at every facility, ensuring that overproduction is never profitable in itself.

For each product at each facility, there is a finite set of production levels, each defining a disjoint interval with a lower bound and an upper bound on the volume of raw materials that may be allocated. These intervals do not overlap, and one of the levels is always a zero level with both bounds equal to zero, representing the option of not producing that product at that facility. The company must select exactly one production level for each product at each facility, and the amount of raw materials allocated must lie within the bounds of the chosen level.

Uncertainty in this problem is captured through a finite set of joint probability distributions for each product, covering both production yield and demand. Each distribution consists of a finite number of equally or unequally weighted scenarios. In each scenario, a yield realization is given for every facility (representing the fraction of allocated raw materials that results in usable finished goods), and a demand realization is given for the product. The key feature of the problem is that uncertainty in production yields is endogenous: the choice of production levels at the facilities determines which joint probability distribution governs the yield and demand outcomes for that product. Specifically, each distribution is associated with a particular combination of production levels across all facilities, defined by a mapping from each product, facility, and distribution to the production level that must be in effect at that facility for that distribution to apply. The company must select exactly one distribution for each product, and if a particular distribution is selected, then the production level indicated by the mapping must be chosen at every facility for that product.

The company must determine: the amount of raw materials of each product to allocate to each facility, the production level to select for each product at each facility, and the distribution to select for each product. Once these planning decisions are made, uncertainty is resolved through the realization of a scenario from the selected distribution. The available finished-goods inventory of each product in each scenario equals the sum over all facilities of the product of the yield realization at that facility and the raw materials allocated there. In each scenario, the company then decides how much of the available inventory to sell at full price and how much to sell at the discounted price. The amount sold at full price cannot exceed the realized demand for that product in that scenario, and the sum of full-price sales and discounted sales must equal the total available inventory.

The goal is to maximize expected profit, defined as the total expected revenue from sales minus total manufacturing costs. Total manufacturing cost equals the sum over all products and facilities of the per-unit manufacturing cost times the raw materials allocated. Expected revenue is computed under the selected distribution for each product: for each scenario in that distribution, revenue equals the full sale price times the quantity sold at full price plus the discounted price times the quantity sold at the discounted price, weighted by the scenario probability. Only the revenue terms from the distribution actually selected for each product contribute to the total expected revenue.
