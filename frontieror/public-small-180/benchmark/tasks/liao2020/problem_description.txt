# Problem Description

Benchmark cost convention: when an instance contains several named cost structures, the first declared structure (`Cost1` in all released instances) is authoritative. The solution repeats its name and its `c_w`, `c_g`, and `c_o` values, and the checker verifies them against the instance.

An outpatient procedure clinic schedules P colonoscopy patients for a single provider within a session of L minutes. Each patient must be assigned to exactly one appointment position in the sequence (positions one through P), and each position receives exactly one patient. The clinic must also determine the scheduled start time for each position. The first position always starts at time zero, every start time must lie between zero and L, and start times must be non-decreasing across positions.

On the day of service, three sources of variability affect operations and are treated as uncertain random parameters: (i) the quality of each patient's bowel preparation, which is either adequate or inadequate; (ii) the procedure duration, which has a known lower and upper bound and a known mean separately for the adequate-preparation case and for the inadequate-preparation case; (iii) each patient's deviation from the scheduled arrival time, which has known lower/upper bounds and a known mean. For each patient p the input data specifies the mean procedure duration with adequate preparation mu_A_p and with inadequate preparation mu_I_p, the lower and upper bounds on duration with adequate preparation d_AL_p and d_AU_p, the lower and upper bounds with inadequate preparation d_IL_p and d_IU_p, the probability of adequate preparation mu_q_p, the mean arrival-time deviation mu_u_p, and the lower and upper bounds on arrival-time deviation u_L_p and u_U_p. Only these per-patient marginal means and support intervals are given; the full joint distribution of preparation quality, procedure durations, and arrival deviations across all patients is NOT known.

After a schedule is realized, each position incurs operational costs as follows. A patient's waiting time at a given position is the amount by which the patient's actual procedure-ready time (the later of its arrival time and the completion of the preceding patient's procedure) exceeds the scheduled start time of that position. A provider idle time between two consecutive positions is the amount by which the scheduled start of the later position exceeds the completion of the earlier position plus any incurred waiting. Provider overtime is the amount by which the completion time of the final position exceeds L. The cost structure specifies a unit waiting cost c_w_i per appointment position i, a unit provider idle cost c_g_i per gap between consecutive positions, and a unit provider overtime cost c_o. The total operational cost for a given realization of uncertainty is the sum over all positions of the unit waiting cost times the waiting time, plus the sum over all gaps of the unit idle cost times the idle time, plus the unit overtime cost times the overtime.

The clinic seeks an appointment assignment and schedule that minimizes the worst-case expected total operational cost, where the worst case is taken over all joint probability distributions of preparation quality, procedure durations, and arrival-time deviations that are consistent with the given patient-level means and support intervals. Equivalently, let the set of distributions consistent with the given marginal means and support intervals form an ambiguity set; the objective is the supremum over distributions in this ambiguity set of the expected operational cost induced by the chosen assignment and schedule. The clinic chooses the assignment and schedule to minimize this worst-case expected cost.
