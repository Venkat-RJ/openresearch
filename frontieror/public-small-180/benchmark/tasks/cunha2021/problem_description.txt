# Problem Description

A supply chain consists of three levels of facilities: a single production plant, a set of warehouses, and a set of retailers. The plant produces a single item that flows downward through the network over a finite planning horizon of discrete time periods. Each warehouse serves a predefined subset of retailers, and each retailer is assigned to exactly one warehouse. Goods can only move from the plant to warehouses and from warehouses to their assigned retailers; transfers between warehouses or between retailers are not permitted. There is no limit on the amount that can be produced at the plant or transported to any facility in any period.

The input data specifies the number of warehouses, the number of retailers, and the number of time periods. A retailer-to-warehouse assignment maps each retailer to its unique warehouse, and equivalently defines for each warehouse the set of retailers it serves. Each retailer has a known demand in each period. The per-period demand at each warehouse is a derived quantity equal to the sum of demands of its assigned retailers in that period, and the per-period demand at the plant is a derived quantity equal to the sum of all retailer demands in that period. Each facility (the plant, each warehouse, and each retailer) has a fixed setup cost for each period, incurred whenever any positive amount is produced at or transported to that facility in that period. Each facility also has a per-unit holding cost for each period, charged on inventory held at that facility at the end of that period. Both fixed setup costs and per-unit holding costs vary by facility and by period.

The planner must determine, for each period, the amount produced at the plant, the amount shipped from the plant to each warehouse, and the amount shipped from each warehouse to each of its assigned retailers, as well as the inventory held at every facility at the end of each period and whether each facility is active (has a positive inflow) in each period.

Inventory at each facility starts at zero at the beginning of the planning horizon. For the plant and each warehouse in each period, the inventory carried from the previous period plus the amount received equals the total amount shipped out to all successor facilities plus the inventory carried into the next period. For each retailer in each period, the inventory carried from the previous period plus the amount received from its warehouse equals the retailer's demand in that period plus the inventory carried into the next period. A facility may receive a positive quantity in a given period only if that facility is set up in that period. The amount received by any facility in a period cannot exceed the total remaining demand that the facility must eventually fulfill from that period through the end of the horizon; specifically, for the plant this is the sum of all retailer demands from the current period onward, for a warehouse it is the sum of demands of all its assigned retailers from the current period onward, and for a retailer it is the sum of its own demands from the current period onward.

The goal is to minimize the total cost over the entire planning horizon, defined as the sum of all fixed setup costs incurred at all facilities in all periods where those facilities are active, plus the sum of all per-unit holding costs for inventory held at all facilities at the end of every period.
