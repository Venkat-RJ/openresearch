# Problem Description

A fleet of vehicles based at a central depot must collect supply from a set of geographically dispersed customers and return to the depot. The fleet is heterogeneous, consisting of multiple truck types and multiple trailer types. Each truck type has a load capacity, a fixed cost, a distance-based cost factor, and a limited number of available units. Each trailer type has its own load capacity, a towing cost multiplier that adds to the truck's travel cost whenever the trailer is attached, and a limited number of available units. A vehicle class is defined by pairing a specific truck type either with nothing (a solo truck) or with a specific trailer type (a complete vehicle). The total number of vehicles of any given truck type or trailer type used across all selected routes must not exceed the number available of that type.

Customers are partitioned into two groups: truck customers and trailer customers. Truck customers have restricted access (small yards or narrow roads) and can only be visited by a truck traveling without its trailer attached. Trailer customers can be visited by either a solo truck or a complete vehicle with the trailer attached. Every customer has a known positive supply quantity that must be collected, a service time, and a location given by coordinates in a two-dimensional plane. Distances and travel times between all pairs of locations (depot, customers, and transshipment locations) are provided.

In addition to the depot and customers, there is a set of dedicated transshipment locations. At any trailer customer location or transshipment location, the driver of a complete vehicle may decouple the trailer and park it. The truck then performs a subtour without the trailer, visiting truck customers and possibly trailer customers, before returning to the parking location to recouple the trailer. At trailer customer locations and transshipment locations where the trailer is parked, load may be transferred from the truck to the trailer. Any number of subtours may be performed from a single parking location, and multiple trailers may be parked at the same location simultaneously.

Each route begins and ends at the depot. For a complete vehicle, the truck must return to the location where its trailer is parked and recouple before returning to the depot. Each customer must be served exactly once across all routes in the solution, and each customer is visited at most once within any single route.

When the trailer is attached, collected supply is loaded into the trailer up to its remaining capacity, and any excess is loaded into the truck. When the truck is operating without its trailer, all collected supply is loaded into the truck. During coupling and transfer operations at parking locations, as much load as possible is moved from the truck to the trailer, keeping the truck as free as possible. No load transfer occurs at decoupling operations. At no point along a route may the truck carry more than its capacity or the trailer carry more than its capacity.

Each customer has a time window specifying the earliest and latest times at which service may begin. The planning horizon defines the time window of the depot, meaning every route must be completed within this horizon. Service at each customer must start within that customer's time window. Service, coupling, decoupling, and transfer operations each consume a fixed amount of time. Travel between locations consumes time equal to the travel time for that pair of locations, which is independent of the vehicle class. The total duration of each route, including all travel, service, and handling times, must fit within the planning horizon.

The cost of traversing an arc between two locations depends on the truck type's cost factor multiplied by the distance, plus, if the trailer is attached during that traversal, the trailer type's towing cost multiplier multiplied by the distance. In addition, coupling operations incur a fixed coupling cost, decoupling operations incur a fixed decoupling cost, and transfer operations may incur a fixed transfer cost. Each truck type also has a fixed cost incurred whenever that truck is used.

The goal is to select a set of routes, one per deployed vehicle, that collectively serve every customer exactly once, respect all capacity, time window, accessibility, and fleet availability restrictions, and minimize the total cost, which is the sum of all fixed vehicle costs, all distance-dependent travel costs (with the trailer towing surcharge where applicable), and all handling costs for coupling, decoupling, and transfer operations.
