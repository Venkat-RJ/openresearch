# Problem Description

A delivery company operates a fleet of identical vehicles from a single depot to serve a set of customers. The depot and each customer occupy known locations in a plane, and every pair of locations has a given nonnegative travel distance and a given nonnegative travel time, both satisfying the triangle inequality. Each customer has a positive weight demand that must be delivered in full, a service time that is incurred once per vehicle visit regardless of the quantity delivered, and a time window defined by an earliest and a latest time at which service may begin. If a vehicle arrives at a customer before the earliest time, it must wait until that time to start service. The depot has its own time window within which all vehicle departures and returns must occur, and its service time is zero.

An unlimited number of vehicles is available, each with the same weight capacity. A customer's demand may exceed the vehicle capacity, and in that case the demand must be split across multiple vehicles. More generally, any customer's demand may be fulfilled by deliveries from one or more vehicles, so split deliveries are permitted for every customer. Each vehicle departs the depot, visits a subset of customers along a route, delivers some quantity of goods at each visited customer, and returns to the depot. A vehicle may visit each customer at most once on its route. The total weight loaded onto a vehicle at the depot equals the sum of all deliveries it will make, and this total may not exceed the vehicle capacity. As the vehicle traverses its route the onboard load decreases with each delivery.

The cost of traversing an edge between two locations depends on both the distance of that edge and the weight the vehicle is carrying on that edge. Specifically, the cost incurred on an edge equals the edge distance multiplied by a linear function of the vehicle's load weight on that edge: the function has the form of a slope parameter times the load weight plus an intercept parameter, where the intercept represents the cost attributable to the vehicle's own curb weight. The slope and intercept are given constants that apply uniformly to all vehicles and all edges.

The planner must determine how many vehicles to dispatch, the route each vehicle follows from the depot through its assigned customers and back, the quantity each vehicle delivers to each customer it visits, and the service start time at each customer for each visiting vehicle. Every customer's full demand must be satisfied by the combined deliveries of all vehicles that visit it. The total quantity carried by any vehicle on any edge of its route must not exceed the vehicle capacity. For each customer visited by a vehicle, the difference between the total weight arriving at that customer across all incoming edges and the total weight leaving across all outgoing edges must account for the delivery made. For each vehicle, the number of times it arrives at any given customer equals the number of times it departs from that customer, and this count is at most one. Every vehicle must leave the depot exactly once and return to the depot exactly once. If a vehicle traverses an edge from one location to another, the service start time at the destination must be no earlier than the service start time at the origin plus the origin's service time plus the travel time of that edge. Service at every location must begin within that location's time window.

The goal is to minimize the total transportation cost, which is the sum over all vehicles and all edges traversed of the edge distance times the linear weight-related cost evaluated at the vehicle's load weight on that edge.
