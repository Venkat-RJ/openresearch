# Problem Description

A manufacturing company operates a two-echelon supply chain consisting of a set of candidate plants and a set of customers over a finite planning horizon divided into discrete periods. The company produces multiple items, and each plant is capable of producing only a specific subset of those items. Similarly, each customer orders only a specific subset of items, and customer demands are specified per item, per customer, and per period, and are known in advance.

The input data specifies, for each combination of item, plant, and period: a per-lot setup cost (charged whenever production of that item takes place at that plant in that period), a per-unit production cost, a per-unit per-period inventory holding cost, a setup time, and a per-unit production time. Each plant has a known capacity in each period, expressed in time units, and a fixed opening cost that is charged once if the plant is used at all during the horizon. For each item, customer, plant, and period, a per-unit transportation cost is given for shipping one unit of that item from that plant to that customer in that period.

The company must make the following decisions: (1) which plants to open for the horizon; (2) for each item, which single plant is responsible for producing that item over the entire horizon; (3) in which periods a production setup for each item is performed at its assigned plant; (4) how much of each item to produce at its assigned plant in each period; and (5) how much inventory of each item to carry from one period to the next at its assigned plant.

Several business rules must be respected. A strict single-sourcing policy applies: each item is produced by exactly one plant, and that plant serves all of that item's demand, for all customers, throughout the entire horizon. An item can only be assigned to a plant that is open and that is capable of producing it. Production of an item at a plant in a given period is allowed only if a setup for that item has been performed at that plant in that period. In every period, the total time consumed at each plant by setups and by production across all items assigned to it must not exceed the plant's capacity in that period, and capacity is only available at plants that are open. Customer demand for each item in each period must be fully satisfied from production in that period or earlier, with any quantity produced before it is needed being carried as inventory at the producing plant; backlogging is not allowed, so cumulative production of each item through each period must cover cumulative demand through that period.

The objective is to minimize total cost, which is the sum of (a) plant opening costs over all opened plants, (b) setup costs over all setups performed, (c) production costs over all units produced, (d) inventory holding costs over all units of inventory carried from one period to the next, and (e) transportation costs for shipping produced units from each item's producing plant to each customer to meet that customer's demand.
