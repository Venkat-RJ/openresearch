# Problem Description

A fleet of identical vehicles, each with a fixed carrying capacity, is stationed at a central depot and must serve a set of customers partitioned into two groups: linehaul customers and backhaul customers. Each linehaul customer has a known non-negative integer demand for goods to be delivered from the depot, and each backhaul customer has a known non-negative integer quantity of goods to be picked up and returned to the depot. The number of linehaul customers, the number of backhaul customers, the number of available vehicles, and the vehicle capacity are all given. A non-negative travel cost is specified for every ordered pair of locations, including the depot and all customers; these costs are generally asymmetric and are organized as a complete cost matrix over all locations.

The task is to design exactly as many routes as there are vehicles, where every route starts and ends at the depot, so that each linehaul customer appears on exactly one route and each backhaul customer appears on exactly one route. Within any single route, all linehaul customers visited on that route must be served before any backhaul customer on that route. A route may serve only linehaul customers with no backhaul customers, but a route serving only backhaul customers with no linehaul customers is not permitted. The total demand of the linehaul customers assigned to any single route must not exceed the vehicle capacity, and, separately, the total demand of the backhaul customers assigned to that same route must also not exceed the vehicle capacity; these two capacity checks are independent of each other because the vehicle delivers all linehaul goods before it begins collecting backhaul goods.

Each route therefore consists of a path from the depot through one or more linehaul customers, followed by a transition arc from the last linehaul customer either directly back to the depot or to a backhaul customer, and then optionally a path through one or more backhaul customers ending at the depot. The cost of a route is the sum of the travel costs along all arcs traversed on that route.

The goal is to minimize the total travel cost across all routes, which equals the sum of the costs of all chosen linehaul paths, plus the costs of all chosen backhaul paths, plus the costs of all transition arcs connecting the linehaul portion of each route to its backhaul portion or to the depot.
