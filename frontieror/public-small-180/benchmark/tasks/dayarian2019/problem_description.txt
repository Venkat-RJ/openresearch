# Problem Description

A catering services company operates a central production facility (depot) and must fulfill a set of customer orders within a single day. The planning horizon is divided into equal-length time periods, and each period is further subdivided into equal-length subperiods. The company produces multiple product types and must simultaneously plan production employee shifts and delivery vehicle routes so that every order is produced and delivered on time, at minimum total cost.

Each order is placed by a customer at a known geographic location and specifies a demand quantity for one or more products, a space requirement representing the physical size of the order for vehicle loading, a service time that a driver must spend at the customer site during delivery, and a time window defined by an earliest and latest allowable delivery time. Products are perishable: each product type has a maximum allowed time between production and delivery, called its life span. Each product type has a known production rate giving the number of units one employee can produce per subperiod. Travel times between every pair of locations (including the depot) are given. Storage at the production facility is not a limiting resource. For each order and each product it demands, a production window is defined by a release time (derived from the order's delivery time window and the product's life span) and a due time (the latest subperiod by which production must be complete so the order can still depart on a trip that meets its time window).

On the production side, this released benchmark uses an aggregate compact staffing model. For every product and subperiod it records an integer number of producing employees and an integer number of setup starts; for every period it records the number of paid workstations and employee-shift starts. Production may be split and preempted. Product output is bounded by its rate times its assigned employees. A rise in a product's employee count requires the corresponding setup starts, setup employees remain occupied for the configured setup duration, and producing plus setting-up employees cannot exceed the employee or paid-workstation limits. Period-level shift-start and minimum-paid-time inequalities determine the paid workforce. These aggregate variables are the released staffing contract; the benchmark does not claim to reconstruct named individual employee trajectories.

On the delivery side, a limited number of indexed vehicle/driver pairs are available. Each used pair operates one route with an active prefix of at most the specified number of trip slots. A trip starts at the depot at the beginning of a period, includes a fixed preparation time, visits the reported ordered customer sequence, and returns to the depot. Capacity and customer time windows are enforced. For multiple trips, the released compact formulation requires only that the later trip start in a strictly later period; it does not impose a continuous-time return-before-next-departure constraint, so trip time intervals may overlap. Route paid time runs from its earliest trip start to the largest modeled customer return bound, and is capped by the maximum route duration. Each vehicle index appears in at most one route.

Every order must be delivered exactly once. Production and delivery are linked by the order's due time: the cumulative quantity of each product produced for an order by the time the order's trip departs the depot must meet the order's demand for that product, and total production of each product for each order across its valid subperiods must at least meet its demand.

The cost of a route is the driver salary (rate per minute times the maximum of modeled route duration and guaranteed minimum paid time) plus vehicle operating cost (rate per minute times exact travel time in the reported customer order). Production cost is the employee salary rate per subperiod times the period-level paid-workstation count, plus setup cost for every certified setup start. The objective is the exact sum of those four components. A submitted solution therefore includes both the ordered routes and the complete aggregate production-staffing certificate needed to recompute this value.
