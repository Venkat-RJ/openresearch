# Problem Description

A company must decide which plants to open from a set of potential facility locations and how to assign customers to those open plants in order to satisfy all customer demands at minimum total cost. There are a given number of customers and a given number of candidate plant sites. Each customer has a known non-negative demand, and each candidate plant has a known strictly positive capacity and a known non-negative fixed cost that is incurred if and only if that plant is opened. For every customer-plant pair, there is a known non-negative shipping cost that represents the cost of supplying that customer's entire demand from that plant. A customer's demand may be split across multiple open plants; specifically, any fraction of a customer's demand between zero and one hundred percent may be served by any single open plant, and the shipping cost for that plant-customer pair scales proportionally with the fraction served. The total capacity across all candidate plants strictly exceeds total customer demand, ensuring that a feasible supply plan exists.

The company must make two types of choices: which plants to open and what fraction of each customer's demand to fulfill from each open plant. Every customer's demand must be met in full, meaning the fractions assigned to that customer across all plants must sum to exactly one. The total demand assigned to each open plant, computed as the sum over all customers of each customer's demand multiplied by the fraction of that demand assigned to the plant, must not exceed that plant's capacity. A closed plant cannot serve any customer at all.

The goal is to minimize total cost, which is the sum of two components: total shipping cost, computed as the sum over all customer-plant pairs of the shipping cost times the fraction of that customer's demand served by that plant, and total fixed cost, computed as the sum of the fixed opening costs of all plants that are chosen to be opened.
