# Problem Description

A network of collaborating hospitals shares a common waiting list of patients requiring surgery and must collectively schedule these patients into operating rooms (ORs) over a multi-day planning horizon. The entities involved are a set of patients, a set of hospitals, a set of days forming the planning horizon, a set of identical ORs within each hospital (every hospital has the same number of ORs), and a set of equally likely scenarios representing uncertain surgery durations. Each patient has a health status score computed as the product of the patient's urgency rating and the difference between the number of days the patient has already been waiting and the number of days in the planning horizon. Patients whose health status score meets or exceeds a given threshold are classified as mandatory; all remaining patients are non-mandatory.

The input data specifies, for each patient: the number of days the patient has been waiting, an urgency rating, the resulting health status score, whether the patient is mandatory, a day-specific scheduling benefit for each day in the horizon, a postponement penalty, and a cancellation penalty. The cancellation penalty depends on whether the patient is mandatory or non-mandatory, with mandatory patients incurring a higher per-unit cancellation cost. For each hospital-day pair, the data specifies the cost of opening that hospital's surgical suite on that day, the cost of opening each individual OR in that hospital on that day, and the operating time limit (in minutes) for each OR on that day. For each scenario, the data provides a realization of every patient's surgery duration in minutes.

The decisions to be made are: which hospital surgical suites to open on which days, which individual ORs to open within each opened hospital on each day, which patients to assign to which specific OR in which hospital on which day, which non-mandatory patients to postpone to a future planning horizon, and -- separately for each uncertainty scenario -- which of the scheduled surgeries to actually perform versus cancel on the day of surgery. These two groups of decisions are made sequentially: the opening, assignment, and postponement decisions are made before surgery durations are revealed, while the cancellation decisions are made afterward, once durations are known for that scenario.

Every mandatory patient must be assigned to exactly one OR at one hospital on one day within the planning horizon. Every non-mandatory patient must either be assigned to exactly one OR at one hospital on one day, or be postponed. A patient can only be assigned to an OR that is open, and an OR can only be open if its hospital's surgical suite is open on that day. Under each scenario, for every open OR, the total realized surgery duration of the patients whose surgeries are actually performed (not cancelled) in that OR must not exceed that OR's operating time limit for that day. A surgery can only be performed in a given scenario if the patient was assigned to that OR in the scheduling stage.

The goal is to minimize the sum of five cost components: the total cost of opening hospital surgical suites, the total cost of opening individual ORs, the total scheduling benefit across all patient-day-OR assignments (which is negative, thereby reducing cost when patients are scheduled earlier and with higher urgency), the total postponement penalty for all non-mandatory patients who are not scheduled, and the expected cancellation cost averaged uniformly over all scenarios, where the cancellation cost in each scenario is the sum over all assigned patients whose surgeries are cancelled of their respective cancellation penalties.
