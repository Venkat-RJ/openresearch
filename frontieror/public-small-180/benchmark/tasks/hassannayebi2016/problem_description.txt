# Problem Description

A rail transit operator must schedule the departure times of a fixed number of train services at a single station over a given planning horizon to minimize total passenger waiting time. The planning horizon spans a known duration in minutes, during which a predetermined number of train services must be dispatched. The planning horizon is divided into a sequence of demand periods, each of equal length in minutes, and each demand period has a known constant passenger arrival rate expressed in passengers per minute. Passengers arrive uniformly within each demand period and accumulate on the platform until a train departs and boards them.

The operator must determine the departure time of each train service within the planning horizon. The planning horizon is partitioned into consecutive headway intervals: an initial interval from the start of the planning horizon to the first departure, one interior interval between each pair of consecutive departures, and a final interval from the last departure to the end of the planning horizon. The length of each such interval is called the headway for that interval. For interior intervals, the headway equals the difference between the departure time of the later train and the departure time of the earlier train. The headway for each interior interval must lie between a specified minimum headway and a specified maximum headway. The initial interval and the final interval are not subject to these minimum and maximum headway bounds. The sum of all headways across all intervals, including the initial and final ones, must equal the total length of the planning horizon.

Each train service must be assigned to exactly one demand period, meaning its departure time falls within that period's time window. The assignment of trains to demand periods must respect chronological ordering: if a given train is assigned to a particular demand period, then the next train in sequence cannot be assigned to any earlier demand period. The departure time of a train assigned to a given period must be at least as large as the start time of that period and strictly less than the start time of the following period.

The cumulative number of passengers who have arrived from the start of the planning horizon up to the departure of each train is computed by summing, over all fully elapsed demand periods before that train's assigned period, the product of each such period's length and its arrival rate, and then adding the product of the elapsed time within the train's own assigned period and that period's arrival rate. For interior intervals, the number of new passenger arrivals during that interval is the difference between the cumulative arrival counts at the later departure and the earlier departure. For the initial interval before the first departure, the number of arriving passengers equals the cumulative arrival count up to the first departure, computed using the same period-assignment and arrival-rate structure as for other trains. For the final interval after the last departure, the number of arriving passengers is computed analogously: it sums, over all demand periods that fully follow the last train's assigned period, the product of each such period's length and its arrival rate, and adds the product of the remaining time within the last train's assigned period (from the last departure to the end of that period) and that period's arrival rate.

The number of waiting passengers after each train departs equals the number waiting after the previous departure, plus the new arrivals during the intervening interval, minus the number who board. The number of passengers who board any single train cannot exceed the train's capacity, which is a given constant. Initially, before the first train departs, there are zero waiting passengers and zero boarding passengers.

The goal is to minimize total passenger waiting time. The total waiting time is the sum, over all headway intervals including the initial interval before the first departure and the final interval after the last departure, of the product of that interval's headway and the estimated platform occupancy during that interval. The platform occupancy during each interval is estimated as the number of waiting passengers at the start of that interval plus half the number of new arrivals during that interval. This expression captures the trapezoidal accumulation of waiting passengers within each interval.

The input data for each instance specifies the total planning horizon length in minutes, the number of train services to schedule, the number of demand periods and the length of each period in minutes, the passenger arrival rate for each demand period in passengers per minute, the minimum and maximum allowed headways in minutes, and the train passenger capacity. The total demand in passengers over the horizon can be derived from the period lengths and arrival rates.
