# Problem Description

A hospital must build a personalized schedule for each of its nurses over a planning horizon of a given number of days. The horizon spans a known number of complete weekends, where each weekend is defined by its constituent Saturday and Sunday (or, in some settings, may include Friday). Each day is divided into a set of named work shifts (such as Early, Late, or Night); in addition, a special rest shift represents a day off. Every nurse possesses a subset of the available skills. The input specifies, for each day, each work shift, and each skill, a minimum number of nurses that must be on duty performing that skill. Each nurse also has a list of individual preferences, where each preference identifies a particular day and either requests a day off or requests a specific shift assignment, together with a numeric penalty incurred if the preference is not honored.

The hospital must assign to each nurse, for every day in the horizon, exactly one shift (which may be a work shift or the rest shift). When a nurse is assigned a work shift on a given day, the nurse must also be assigned exactly one skill to perform during that shift. A cost is incurred for each such assignment where the nurse does not possess the assigned skill; depending on the problem instance, skill possession may be a hard requirement or a soft requirement with the stated finite penalty. Normally skill staffing plus unsatisfied-demand slack must be at least the requested demand. When `demand_equality=true`, staffing must equal demand exactly and unmet-demand slack is disabled.

Several rules govern each nurse's schedule, and each rule may be designated as either hard (must be satisfied) or soft (may be violated at a cost). When a rule is soft, separate penalties apply per unit of violation above the upper bound and per unit of violation below the lower bound, and these two penalties may differ. Hard rules are enforced by assigning a prohibitively large penalty. The rules are as follows.

For each designated group of shifts (which may be all work shifts, a single specific shift such as Night, the rest shift, or any other specified subset of shifts), the total number of days on which each nurse is assigned a shift from that group over the entire horizon must lie between a specified minimum and maximum. The total number of weekends on which each nurse works must lie between a specified minimum and maximum. For each designated group of shifts, the number of consecutive days in any unbroken streak must lie between the stated bounds; streaks touching the horizon boundary are not penalized below the minimum because they may continue outside the window. The number of consecutive weekends worked is likewise bounded. When the identical-weekend rule is enabled, all days named by the weekend record must carry the same assignment: this covers Friday, Saturday, and Sunday in a three-day-weekend instance and Saturday-Sunday otherwise. The schedule must avoid hard forbidden patterns and pays the stated cost for soft forbidden patterns. Each nurse's per-day preferences contribute their own penalty when unmet.

The goal is to determine the shift assignment and skill assignment for every nurse on every day so as to minimize the total cost. The total cost is the sum of three components: penalties for violations of the roster rules listed above (total shift-group counts, weekend-work counts, consecutive-assignment bounds, consecutive-weekend bounds, identical-weekend requirements, forbidden-pattern violations, and unmet nurse preferences), penalties for nurse-skill assignments where the nurse does not possess the assigned skill, and penalties for unsatisfied demand across all day-shift-skill combinations.

The public answer is a complete two-part certificate. `schedule` names one
shift for every nurse-day and `skill_assignments` names exactly one skill on
every worked day (`None` on days off). The checker derives staffing, all
hard/soft penalties, preferences, skill mismatches, and the full objective from
these maps; a missing or extra nurse-day entry is rejected.
