# Problem Description

A wind energy developer plans an onshore wind farm over a region containing three types of locations: grid substations, potential turbine sites, and optional intermediate routing points (called Steiner nodes). The substations are fixed connection points to the electrical grid. The potential turbine sites are candidate locations where a wind turbine may be installed. The Steiner nodes exist solely to provide additional flexibility for cable routing between turbines and substations; they cannot host turbines and produce no energy. The region is represented as an undirected graph whose nodes are the union of all substations, potential turbine sites, and Steiner nodes, and whose edges are the feasible cable connections between pairs of nodes.

Each potential turbine site has an installation cost, an annual energy yield, and a scenic impact value reflecting the visual burden that placing a turbine there would impose on the surrounding landscape. Each edge has a cable cost representing the expense of laying that cable segment, and a scenic impact value representing the visual burden of that cable route on the landscape. Substations and Steiner nodes carry no installation cost, no energy yield, and no scenic impact. All substations are mutually interconnected by edges of zero cost and zero scenic impact, reflecting their shared connection to the existing power grid.

The developer must decide which subset of potential turbine sites to build and which cable connections to lay. The chosen cables, together with the built turbine sites, the substations, and any Steiner nodes used for routing, must form a single connected tree (no cycles, no disconnected components). Every substation must lie on this tree. A potential turbine site is considered built if and only if it lies on the tree; any candidate site not on the tree is not built. The total annual energy yield of the built turbine sites must meet or exceed a prescribed minimum quota.

The problem has two competing goals: total financial cost and total scenic impact. The total financial cost is the sum of the cable costs of all edges included in the tree plus the installation costs of all built turbine sites. The total scenic impact is the sum of the scenic impact values of all edges included in the tree plus the scenic impact values of all built turbine sites. These two goals are combined into a single weighted sum using a parameter alpha between zero and one inclusive: the weighted sum equals alpha times the total financial cost plus one minus alpha times the total scenic impact. When alpha equals one the problem considers only financial cost; when alpha equals zero it considers only scenic impact. The goal is to minimize this weighted sum while meeting the energy yield quota.

To summarize the input data: the graph specifies for every node its type (substation, potential turbine, or Steiner node), its spatial coordinates, its installation cost (positive only for potential turbines), its annual energy yield (positive only for potential turbines), and its scenic impact (positive only for potential turbines); for every edge it specifies the two endpoints, the cable cost, and the scenic impact; and globally the instance specifies the minimum energy quota and the weighting parameter alpha. The decisions to be made are which potential turbine sites to build and which edges to include, such that the selected edges and nodes form a tree containing every substation, the total energy yield of chosen turbines meets the quota, and the weighted combination of financial cost and scenic impact is minimized.
