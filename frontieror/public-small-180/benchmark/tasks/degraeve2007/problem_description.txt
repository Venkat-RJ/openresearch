# Problem Description

A manufacturing facility produces multiple products over a finite planning horizon divided into discrete time periods. All products share a single production resource that has a limited available capacity in each period, measured in units of time. The input data specifies, for each product and each period, the demand that must be satisfied, a per-unit holding cost for carrying inventory from one period to the next, a fixed setup cost incurred whenever production of that product takes place in that period, a per-unit variable production cost, a setup time consumed by initiating production of that product in that period, and a per-unit variable production time. Each period also has a known total capacity. In addition, each product has an associated per-unit initial inventory cost, which is a large penalty cost used to ensure that a valid plan can always be constructed.

The planner must determine, for each product in each period, whether to set up for production of that product, how much of that product to produce, and how much inventory of that product to carry forward to the next period. The planner must also determine, for each product, an amount of initial inventory that is available at the start of the first period without requiring a setup.

Demand must be met in every period for every product. For the first period, the initial inventory of a product plus the production of that product in the first period must equal the demand for that product in the first period plus the ending inventory of that product at the end of the first period. For every subsequent period, the inventory of a product carried from the previous period plus the production of that product in the current period must equal the demand for that product in the current period plus the ending inventory of that product at the end of the current period.

Production of a product in a given period can only occur if a setup for that product has been performed in that period. Furthermore, the quantity produced of a product in any period is bounded above by the smaller of two quantities: the remaining capacity in that period after accounting for the setup time divided by the per-unit production time, and the cumulative remaining demand for that product from that period through the end of the planning horizon.

In each period, the total time consumed across all products by setups and production must not exceed the period's capacity. Specifically, for each product that is set up in a period, the setup time is consumed, and for each unit produced of any product, the corresponding variable production time is consumed, and the sum of all these times across all products must remain within the period's capacity.

The goal is to minimize total cost, which is the sum of four components: the total initial inventory cost across all products, the total setup costs incurred across all products and periods, the total variable production costs across all products and periods, and the total inventory holding costs across all products and periods.
