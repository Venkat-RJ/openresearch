# Problem Description

A distribution company operates a fleet of identical vehicles from multiple depots to serve a set of geographically dispersed customers over a planning horizon of several days (periods). The company has a given number of depots and a given number of customers, each located at known coordinates in the plane. A fixed number of identical vehicles is stationed at each depot. Each vehicle has the same carrying capacity and is subject to the same maximum route duration, where route duration is computed as the sum of all travel times and all service times along the route.

The input data specifies the following for every customer: a two-dimensional location, a demand quantity to be delivered on each visit, a service duration representing the time spent at the customer site, a service frequency indicating how many times the customer must be visited over the planning horizon, and a list of feasible visit patterns, where each pattern is a specific subset of days on which visits may occur. For example, a customer with frequency two and a single feasible pattern consisting of days one and two must be visited on both of those days. The input also specifies the location of each depot and the travel cost (travel time) between every pair of locations, including depot-to-depot, depot-to-customer, and customer-to-customer pairs. The travel costs are defined on a complete graph connecting all depots and all customers. Service time at depots is zero.

The company must make the following decisions. First, each customer must be assigned to exactly one depot and exactly one of its feasible visit patterns. This assignment is joint: a single choice determines both which depot will serve the customer and on which days that customer will be visited. Once assigned, all visits to a given customer across all periods must originate from the same depot. Second, for each depot and each day, the company must design vehicle routes that begin at the depot, visit a subset of customers, and return to the same depot. Each route is operated by a single vehicle.

The routes must satisfy several rules. Each vehicle may depart from its home depot at most once per day. A vehicle based at one depot may never depart from or pass through a different depot; routes from a given depot only serve customers assigned to that depot. On each day, a customer assigned to a pattern that includes that day must appear on exactly one route originating from the customer's assigned depot; a customer whose pattern does not include that day must not be visited at all on that day. Every route must form a simple tour starting and ending at its depot, meaning no customer may appear more than once on the same route and no subset of two or more customers may form a disconnected loop. For every vehicle on every day, the total demand of all customers served on its route must not exceed the vehicle capacity, and the total duration of the route, measured as the sum of travel times between consecutive stops plus the service times at all visited customers, must not exceed the maximum route duration.

The goal is to minimize the total travel cost, computed as the sum of travel costs over all arcs traversed by all vehicles across all depots and all days of the planning horizon.
